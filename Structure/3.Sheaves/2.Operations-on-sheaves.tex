\chapter{Operations on sheaves}\label{ch:operations-on-sheaves}

In this chapter we introduce the operations used to change the base space of a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} along a continuous map \(f\colon X \to Y\)

We begin by constructing the direct image (or pushforward) \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(f_{\ast}\) --- which allows us to push data from \(X\) to \(Y\) --- and the inverse image (or pullback) \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(f^{-1}\) --- which pulls data back from \(Y\) to \(X\) --- and prove that they are adjoint.

As a particular case we will study how taking one of the spaces to be \(\{\ast\}\) we recover classical \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} constructions such as the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk}.
We also study the case for open and closed immersions, where as we will see another \hyperref[def:adjoint-functor]{adjoint functor} exists and prove that these are connected via a canonical \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence}.

The main references for this chapter are \cite{stacks-project, kashiwaraSheavesManifolds1990}.

\section{Continuous maps and (pre)sheaves}\label{sec:continuous-maps-sheaves}

Consider a continuous map between topological spaces \(f\colon X \to Y\).

We want to find a way of translating \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaves} and \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves} from \(X\) to \(Y\) along \(f\) and viceversa.
We will start in the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} case and move to the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} case using \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification} when needed.

First, let's see how we can push forward data from \(X\) to \(Y\).
This will be easy since for any open subset \(V \subseteq Y\) we have that \(f^{-1}(V)\) is open in \(X\).
\begin{definition}[Direct image presheaf]\label{def:direct-image-sheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map.
  For any \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} over \(X\) we define the \textbf{direct image} (or pushforward) of \(\mathcal{F}\) via \(f\) as the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} \(f_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\) over \(Y\) which, for any open subset \(V \subseteq Y\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left(f_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\right)(V) \coloneq \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(V))
  \end{equation*}
  and for any \(V_{1} \subseteq V_{2} \subseteq Y\) the restriction is given by
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      f_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}(V_{2})) \arrow[d, dotted] \arrow[r, equal] & \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(V_{2})) \arrow[d] \\
      f_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}(V_{1})) \arrow[r, equal] & \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(V_{1}))
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  Note that this defines a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    f_{\ast}\colon \PSh_{X}(\category{C}) \to \PSh_{Y}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

Since taking inverse images of open sets behaves nicely under composition we have that \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct images} compose as expected: if \(f\colon X \to Y\) and \(g: Y \to Z\) be two continuous maps then the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct images} \((g \circ f)_{\ast}\) and \(g_{\ast} \circ f_{\ast}\) are equal.

Furthermore using that \(f^{-1}(\bigcup_{i} V_{i}) = \bigcup_{i} f^{-1}(V_{i})\) we have directly that if \(\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} then \(f_{\ast}\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}.

This means that we have a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}
\begin{equation*}
  f_{\ast}\colon \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{Y}(\category{C})
\end{equation*}

Let's try now pulling back data from \(Y\) to \(X\).
Note that in this case if \(U \subseteq X\) is open \(f(U) \subseteq Y\) is not necessarily open in \(Y\).
Therefore, we have to \textit{aproximate} the data by looking at all open sets in \(Y\) that contain \(f(U)\).
\begin{definition}[Inverse image presheaf]\label{def:inverse-image-presheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map between two topological spaces \(X, Y\).
  For any \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} \(\mathcal{G}\) over \(Y\) we define the \textbf{inverse image} (or pullback) of \(\mathcal{G}\) via \(f\) as the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} \(f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G}\) over \(X\) which, for any open subset \(U \subseteq X\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)(U) \coloneq \varinjlim_{\substack{V \supseteq f(U) \\ V \in \Open(Y) }} \mathcal{G}(V)
  \end{equation*}

  Since this is a \hyperref[def:direct-limit]{direct limit} following \autoref{ex:direct-limits-set} we may describe \(\mathcal{F}_{p}\) as
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)(U) = \quot{\{(s, V) \mid f(U) \subseteq V \in \Open(X) \land s \in \mathcal{G}(V)\}}{\sim}
  \end{equation*}
  where
  \begin{equation*}
    (s, V) \sim (t, W) \iff \exists Z \subseteq V \cap W \mid f(U) \subseteq Z \in \Open(Y) \land s|_{Z} = t|_{Z}
  \end{equation*}

  In this way, if \(U_{1} \subseteq U_{2} \subseteq X\) are open subsets of \(X\) the restriction is given by
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{res}_{U_{2}, U_{1}} \left(f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)([s, V]) = [(s, V)]
  \end{equation*}
  
  Note that this defines a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(f_{\pre}^{-1}\colon \PSh_{Y}(\category{C}) \to \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\).
\end{definition}

\begin{notation}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(f\colon Y \hookrightarrow X\) an inclussion of a set \(Y \subseteq X\) endowed with the induced topology.
  If \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} (resp. \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf}) over \(X\) we call \(f^{-1}\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{Y}(\category{C})\) (resp. \(j_{\pre}^{-1}\sheaf{G}\)) the \textbf{restriction} of \(\sheaf{G}\) to \(Y\) and denote it by \(\sheaf{G}|_{Y}\).
\end{notation}


Since \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} are particular cases of \hyperref[def:direct-limit]{direct limits}, \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{inverse images} play nicely with them.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-inverse-image-presheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map.
  Let \(\mathcal{G} \in \PSh_{Y}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} over \(Y\).
  Then for all \(p \in X\) there is a bijection of \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}.
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)_{p} \cong \mathcal{G}_{f(p)}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \begin{align*}
    \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)_{p} &= \varinjlim_{\substack{U \ni p \\ U \in \Open(X)}}  \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \right)(U) \\
                                      &= \varinjlim_{\substack{U \ni p \\ U \in \Open(X)}} \left( \varinjlim_{\substack{V \supseteq f(U) \\ V \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{G}(V) \right) \\
                                      &= \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni f(p) \\ V \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{G}(V) \\
                                      &= \mathcal{G}_{f(p)} \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

Therefore since \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness can be checked at the level of stalks} we have:
\begin{corollary}
  Let \(\category{C}\) be a \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian category}.
  For all continuous maps \(f\colon X \to Y\) the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{presheaf inverse image} \(f_{\pre}^{-1}: \PSh_{Y}(\category{C}) \to \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
\end{corollary}

Although the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{definition of the inverse image} may seem complicated at first sight it is natural in the sense that it makes \(f_{\ast}\) and \(f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}\) \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjoint}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-presheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous function between two topological spaces \(X, Y\).
  Let \(\category{C}\) be a \hyperref[def:category]{category}.
  Then there exists an \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}: \PSh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, shift right, "f_{\ast}"'] &  \PSh_{Y}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, shift right, "f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  We want to construct \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural transformations}
  \begin{align*}
    \eta\colon \id_{\PSh_{Y}(\category{C})} &\implies f_{\ast} \circ f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \\
    \varepsilon\colon f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \circ f_{\ast} &\implies \id_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})}
  \end{align*}
  making for all \(\mathcal{F} \in \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\) and \(\mathcal{G} \in \PSh_{Y}(\category{C})\) the following diagrams commute
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}) \arrow[r, "\id_{\mathcal{G}}"] \arrow[d, "f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}(\eta_{\mathcal{G}})"'] & f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}) \\
      \left( f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \circ f_{\ast} \circ f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \right)(\mathcal{G}) \arrow[ur, "\varepsilon_{f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})}"']
    \end{tikzcd}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      f_{\ast}(\mathcal{G}) \arrow[r, "\id_{\mathcal{F}}"] \arrow[d, "\eta_{f_{\ast}(\mathcal{F})}"'] & f_{\ast}(\mathcal{G}) \\
      \left( f_{\ast} \circ f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \circ f_{\ast} \right)(\mathcal{F}) \arrow[ur, "f_{\ast}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}})"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  Note that, by definition of the \hyperref[def:direct-image-presheaf]{direct image presheaf} and \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{inverse image presheaf} we have, for \(U \in \Open(X)\) and \(V \in \Open(Y)\)
  \begin{align*}
    \left( f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \circ f_{\ast} \right)(\mathcal{F})(U) &= \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq f(U) \\ W \in \Open(Y)}} (f_{\ast}\mathcal{F})(W) = \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq f(U) \\ W \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(W)) \\
    \left( f_{\ast} \circ f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1} \right)(\mathcal{G})(V) &= (f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}\mathcal{G})(f^{-1}(V)) = \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq (f \circ f^{-1})(V) \\ W \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{G}(W) \end{align*}

  Since we have \((f \circ f^{-1})(V) \subseteq V\), we can define \(\eta_{\mathcal{G}}(V)\) as the canonical injection into the \hyperref[def:direct-limit]{direct limit}
  \begin{equation*}
    \eta_{\mathcal{G}}(V)\colon \mathcal{G}(V) \to \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq (f \circ f^{-1})(V) \\ W \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{G}(W)
  \end{equation*}

  On the other hand, since for every \(W \subseteq f(U)\) open we have \(f^{-1}(W) \subseteq (f^{-1} \circ f)(U) \subseteq U\) there exists a restriction morphism
  \begin{equation*}
    \res_{f^{-1}(W), U}\colon \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(W)) \to \mathcal{F}(U)
  \end{equation*}
  Using these maps we obtain a morphism
  \begin{equation*}
    \varepsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(U)\colon \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq f(U) \\ W \in \Open(Y)}} \mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(W)) \to \mathcal{F}(U) \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}

Using this \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction} we can see that the composition works as expected.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:composition-inverse-image-presheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) and \(g\colon Y \to Z\) be continuous maps.
  Then the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{inverse images} \((g \circ f)_{\pre}^{-1}\) and \(f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ g_{\pre}^{-1}\) coincide (up to \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Using the \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-presheaf]{adjunction \(f_{\pre}^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\)} we have, on the one hand
  \begin{equation*}
    (g \circ f)_{\pre}^{-1} \dashv (g \circ f)_{\ast} = g_{\ast} \circ f_{\ast}
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand note that
  \begin{equation*}
    f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ g_{\pre}^{-1} \dashv g_{\ast} \circ f_{\ast}
  \end{equation*}
  Indeed
  \begin{align*}
    \Hom_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( \left( f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ g_{\pre}^{-1} \right) (\mathcal{H}), \mathcal{F} \right) &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{Y}(\category{C})} \left( g_{\pre}^{-1}(\mathcal{H}), f_{\ast}(\mathcal{F}) \right) \\
                                                                                                   &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{Z}(\category{C})} \left( \mathcal{H}, \left( g_{\ast} \circ f_{\ast} \right)(\mathcal{F}) \right)
  \end{align*}

  Therefore since \hyperref[prop:uniqueness-adjoints]{adjoints are unique} we have \((g \circ f)_{\pre}^{-1} \cong f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ g_{\pre}^{-1}\).
\end{proof}

Extending the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{inverse image} to the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} case is not as easy as with the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image} since \(f_{\operatorname{pre}}^{-1}\sheaf{F}\) is not necessarily a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}.
Therefore we need to \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafificate}.
\begin{definition}\label{def:inverse-image-sheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map.
  The \textbf{inverse image} (or pullback) \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} via \(f\) is the \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    f^{-1} \coloneq -^{\#} \circ f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ \iota: \Sh_{Y}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
  where \(-^{\#}\) is the \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification functor}, \(f_{\pre}^{-1}\) is the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{inverse image of presheaves} and \(\iota\colon \Sh_{Y}(\category{C}) \hookrightarrow \PSh_{Y}(\category{C})\) is the forgetful \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}.
\end{definition}

Using the \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification adjunction} we still have the \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction} \(f^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\).
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-sheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map
  There exists an \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      f^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}: \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, shift right, "f_{\ast}"'] & \Sh_{Y}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, shift right, "f^{-1}"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  between the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct} and \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse} images of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Using the \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunctions} \(-^{\#} \dashv \iota\) and \(f_{\pre}^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\) we have
  \begin{align*}
    \Hom_{\Sh_{X}(\category{C})}(f^{-1}(\sheaf{G}), \sheaf{F}) &= \Hom_{\Sh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( \left( -^{\#} \circ f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ \iota \right)(\sheaf{G}), \sheaf{F} \right) \\
                                                                 &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})}((f_{\pre}^{-1} \circ \iota)(\sheaf{G}), \iota(\sheaf{F}))  \\
                                                                  &= \Hom_{\PSh_{Y}(\category{C})}(\iota(\sheaf{G}), (f_{\ast} \circ \iota)(\sheaf{F})) \\
                                                                  &= \Hom_{\PSh_{Y}(\category{C})}(\iota(\sheaf{G}), (\iota \circ f_{\ast})(\sheaf{F})) \\
                                                                  &= \Hom_{\Sh_{Y}(\category{C})}(\sheaf{G}, f_{\ast}(\sheaf{F})) \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

Analogously as in \autoref{lemma:composition-inverse-image-sheaf} we see that composition behaves as expected.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:composition-inverse-image-sheaf}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) and \(g\colon Y \to Z\) be continuous maps.
  Then the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse images} \((g \circ f)^{-1}\) and \(f^{-1} \circ g^{-1}\) coincide (up to \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}).
\end{lemma}

\hyperref[def:stalk]{Stalk} computation also works fine.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-inverse-image}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a continuous map.
  Let \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{Y}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} over \(Y\).
  Then for all \(p \in X\) there is a bijection of \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}.
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( f^{-1}\sheaf{G} \right)_{p} \cong \sheaf{G}_{f(p)}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \begin{align*}
    \left( f^{-1}\sheaf{G} \right)_{p} &= \left( \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}(\iota\sheaf{G}) \right)^{\#} \right)_{p} & \left( \text{definition} \right)\\
                                       &= \left( f_{\pre}^{-1}(\iota\sheaf{G}) \right)_{p} & \left( \text{\autoref{lemma:stalk-commute-sheafification}} \right) \\
                                       &= (\iota\sheaf{G})_{f(p)} & \left( \text{\autoref{lemma:stalk-inverse-image-presheaf}} \right) \\
                                       &= \sheaf{G}_{f(p)} & \left( \text{\autoref{lemma:sheafification-of-sheaf-is-sheaf} and \autoref{lemma:stalk-commute-sheafification}} \right) \quad \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

Thus as in the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} case since \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness can be checked at the level of stalks} we have:
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact}
  Let \(\category{C}\) be a \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian category}.
  For all continuous maps \(f: X \to Y\) the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{sheaf inverse image} \(f^{-1}\colon \Sh_{Y}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
\end{corollary}

\section{Particular cases}

\subsection{Sheaves over a point}

To gain a little bit more intuition for these operations, we are going to study them when one of our spaces is the one-point topological space \(\{\ast\}\).
Recall that in this case we have \hyperref[ex:one-point-sheaves-are-category]{\(\Sh_{{\ast}}(\category{C}) \simeq \category{C}\)}.

We will see that some of the most common \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf constructions} can be seen as particular cases of \(f_{\ast}\) and \(f^{-1}\) in this case.

Let's consider first the following continuous map:
\begin{align*}
  a_{X}\colon& X \to \{\ast\} \\
        & x \mapsto \ast
\end{align*}
This produces a pair of \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjoint functors}
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
    \category{C} \arrow[r, "a_{X}^{-1}"{name=A}, bend left=40] & \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, "a_{X, \ast}"{name=B}, bend left=40]
  \arrow[phantom, from=A, to=B, "\perp"] % \arrow[r, "i_{p}^{-1}"{name=U}, bend left=40] & \category{C} \arrow[l, "i_{p, \ast}"{name=D}, bend left=40]
    % \arrow[phantom, from=U, to=D, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

\begin{definition}[Global sections]\label{def:global-sections-sheaf}
  The \textbf{global sections} \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} is
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma(X, -) \coloneq a_{X, \ast}\colon \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \to \category{C}
  \end{equation*}

  Less abstractly, it sends a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) to \(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}) = \sheaf{F}(X)\)
  and a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{morphism of sheaves} \(\phi: \sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{G}\) to \(\Gamma(X, \phi) = \sheaf{F}(X) \xrightarrow{\phi(X)} \sheaf{G}(X)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{notation}
  If \(Y \subseteq X\) is a subset of \(X\) and \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) we set \(\Gamma(Y, \sheaf{F}) \coloneq \Gamma(Y, \sheaf{F}|_{Y})\).
\end{notation}

\begin{definition}[Constant sheaf]\label{def:constant-sheaf}
  The \textbf{constant sheaf} \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} is
  \begin{equation*}
    \underline{-}_{X} \coloneq a_{X}^{-1}\colon \category{C} \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}

  Less abstractly, it sends an object \(A \in \category{C}\) to the sheaf \(\underline{A}_{X}\) whose sections over an open set \(U \subseteq X\) are the locally constant functions from \(U\) to \(A\) (viewing \(A\) as being equipped with the discrete topology).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:constant-sheaf-sheafification}
  The formulation \(\underline{A}_{X} = a_{X}^{-1}(A)\) is equivalent to the standard construction of the constant sheaf via \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification}.

  Indeed, the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{presheaf inverse image} of \(A\) along \(a_{X}\) assigns to any open \(U \subseteq X\) the \hyperref[def:categorical-colimit]{colimit} over open neighborhoods of \(a_{X}(U) = \{\ast\}\). Since the only neighborhood is \(\{\ast\}\) itself, this \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} is exactly the \hyperref[def:constant-presheaf]{constant presheaf} \(U \mapsto A\). The \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse image functor} \(a_{X}^{-1}\) then applies \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification}, yielding the \hyperref[def:constant-sheaf]{constant sheaf}.
\end{remark}

From \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-sheaf]{the adjunction \(f^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\)}.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:global-constant-adjunction-sheaf}
  There is an \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
    \category{C} \arrow[r, "{\underline{-}_{X}}"{name=A}, bend left=40] & \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, "{\Gamma(X, -)}"{name=B}, bend left=40]
    \arrow[phantom, from=A, to=B, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
\end{corollary}


Using this adjunction by \autoref{ex:adjoint-functors-and-exactness} we have that \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{left exact}.
However as the next example shows it is in general not \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{right exact}.
\begin{example}
  Let \(X \coloneq \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}\) and consider \(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}\) the \hyperref[def:constant-sheaf]{constant sheaf} of integers, \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\) the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} of holomorphic functions and \(\sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast}\) the  \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} of non-vanishing holomorphic functions on \(X\).

  Let \(\exp\colon \sheaf{O}_{X} \to \sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast}\) be the map that sends an holomorphic function \(f\) on an open set \(U\) to \(e^{2\pi i f}\).
  We have that
  \begin{equation*}
    0 \to \underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X} \to \sheaf{O}_{X} \xrightarrow{\exp} \sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast} \to 0
  \end{equation*}
  is a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence} of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves}.

  Indeed it is \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact} at \(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}\) and \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\) since \(e^{2\pi i f} = 1\) if and only if \(f\) is locally constant to an integer and thus \(\ker \exp = \underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}\).
  \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{Exactness} at \(\sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast}\) is direct since \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness can be checked locally} and for all \(p \in X\) there exists an holomorphic branch of the logarithm in a neighbourhood of \(p\).

  However, the induced sequence
  \begin{equation*}
    0 \to \Gamma(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}) \to \Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X}) \xrightarrow{\Gamma\exp} \Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast}) \to 0
  \end{equation*}
  is not \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact} since if we consider \(\id_{X} \in \sheaf{O}_{X}^{\ast}\) this would imply that there exists a function \(g \in \sheaf{O}_{X}\) such that \(e^{2\pi i f(z)} = g(z)\) for all \(z\), i.e: \(g\) would be a global holomorphic branch of the logarithm which cannot exist.
\end{example}

This failure of exactness is exactly what motivates the study of sheaf cohomology.

Now given a point \(p \in X\) let's consider
\begin{align*}
  i_{p}\colon& \{\ast\} \to X \\
        & \ast \mapsto p
\end{align*}
As before this gives rise to an \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
    \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, "i_{p}^{-1}"{name=U}, bend left=40] & \category{C} \arrow[l, "i_{p, \ast}"{name=D}, bend left=40] \arrow[phantom, from=U, to=D, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}


\begin{definition}[Skyscraper sheaf]\label{def:skyscraper-sheaf}
  The \textbf{skyscraper sheaf} \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} is
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{sk}_{p} \coloneq i_{p, \ast}\colon \category{C} \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}

  Less abstractly, it sends an object \(A \in \category{C}\) to the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}
  \begin{equation*}
    (\operatorname{sk}_{p}A)(U) = \begin{cases}
      A & p \in U \\
      1 & p \notin U
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  for all open subsets \(U \subseteq X\) with restriction maps for \(V \subseteq U\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \res_{U, V} = \begin{cases}
      \id_{A} & p \in V \\
      \id_{A} & p \notin U \\
      A \to 1 & p \in U \land p \notin V
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

The last \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(i_{p}^{-1}\) is one we have already seen.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-as-inverse-image}
  For all points \(p \in X\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    -_{p} \cong i_{p}^{-1}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Identifying \hyperref[ex:one-point-sheaves-are-category]{\(\Sh_{\{\ast\}}(\category{C}) \simeq \category{C}\)} if \(\mathcal{F} \in \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\) is a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( i_{p, \pre}^{-1} \mathcal{F} \right)(\{\ast\}) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \supseteq i_{p}(\{\ast\}) \\ V \in \Open(X)}} \mathcal{F}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ V \in \Open(X)}} \mathcal{F}(V) = \mathcal{F}_{p}
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore if \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} we have
  \begin{align*}
    \left( i_{p}^{-1}\sheaf{F} \right)(\{\ast\}) &= \left( -^{\#} \circ i_{p, \pre}^{-1} \circ \iota \right)(\sheaf{F})(\{\ast\}) \\
                                              &= \left( \left( \iota(\sheaf{F}) \right)^{\#} \right)_{p} \\
                                              &= \left( \iota(\sheaf{F}) \right)_{p} \\
                                              &= \sheaf{F}_{p} \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  From \autoref{cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact} we have that the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk functor} is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}, which gives another proof of \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness being checked at the level of stalks} using \autoref{lemma:characterization-exact-functor}.
\end{remark}

As in the other case, directly from the \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-sheaf]{the adjunction \(f^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\)}.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:skyscraper-stalk-adjunction-sheaf}
  There is an \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
    \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, "{-_{p}}"{name=A}, bend left=40] & \category{C} \arrow[l, "{\operatorname{sk}_{p}}"{name=B}, bend left=40]
    \arrow[phantom, from=A, to=B, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
\end{corollary}

We begin by giving a more explicit description for both the sections and \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} of the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse image}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:inverse-image-open-map}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let \(\mathcal{G} \in \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} over \(X\).

          Then \(j_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G} \in \PSh_{U}(\category{C})\) is given by, for any open subset \(V \subseteq U\)
          \begin{equation*}
            j_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G}(V) = \mathcal{G}(V)
          \end{equation*}
          Furthermore \(j_{\pre}^{-1} \circ j_{\ast} = \id_{\PSh_{U}(\category{C})}\).
    \item Let \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} over \(X\).

          Then \(j^{-1}\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{U}(\category{C})\) is given by, for any subset \(V \subseteq U\)
          \begin{equation*}
            j^{-1}\sheaf{G}(V) = \sheaf{G}(V)
          \end{equation*}
          Furthermore \(j^{-1} \circ j_{\ast} = \id_{\Sh_{U}(\category{C})}\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By \hyperref[def:inverse-image-presheaf]{definition} we have
  \begin{equation*}
    j_{\pre}^{-1}\mathcal{G}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq j(V) \\ W \in \Open(U)}} \mathcal{G}(W) =  \varinjlim_{\substack{W \supseteq V \\ W \in \Open(U)}} \mathcal{G}(W)
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore since \(V\) is open this last \hyperref[def:direct-limit]{direct limit} is exactly \(\mathcal{G}(V)\).

  Furthermore if \(\mathcal{F} \in \PSh_{U}(\category{C})\) for any open subset \(V \subseteq U\) we have
  \begin{align*}
    \left( j_{\pre}^{-1} \circ j_{\ast} \right)\mathcal{F}(V) = j_{\pre}^{-1} \mathcal{F}(j^{-1}(V)) = \mathcal{F}(j^{-1}(V)) = \mathcal{F}(V)
  \end{align*}

  The statement about \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves} follows directly since \(V \mapsto \sheaf{G}(V)\) is clearly a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} if \(\sheaf{G}\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:inverse-image-stalk-open-map}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion
  Let \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} over \(X\).
  For all \(p \in U\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j^{-1}\sheaf{G} \right)_{p} = \sheaf{G}_{p}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \begin{align*}
    \left( j^{-1}\sheaf{G} \right)_{p} &= \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ V \in \Open(U)}} j^{-1}\sheaf{G}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ V \in \Open(U)}} \sheaf{G}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ V \in \Open(X)}} \sheaf{G}(V) = \sheaf{G}_{p} \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

This characterization allows us to construct another \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjoint functor} \(j_{!}\) (pronunced \(j\) lower ``shriek'') to \(j^{-1}\), in this case with \(j^{-1} \dashv j_{!}\).
Let's do it first for the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} case.

\begin{definition}\label{def:extension-by-zero-presheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  Let \(\mathcal{F} \in \PSh_{U}(\category{C})\) be a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} over \(U\).
  Suppose that \(\category{C}\) has an \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e \in \category{C}\).

  The \textbf{extension} \(j_{!}^{\pre} \mathcal{F}\) of \(\mathcal{F}\) by \(e\) is the \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} over \(X\) given by, for any open subset \(V \subseteq X\)
  \begin{equation*}
    j_{!}^{\pre} \mathcal{F}(V) \coloneq \begin{cases}
      e & V \not\subseteq U \\
      \mathcal{F}(V) & V \subseteq U
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  The restriction maps are given by, for any \(W \subseteq V \subseteq X\) open subsets
  \begin{equation*}
    \res_{V, W} \coloneq \begin{cases}
      \res_{V, W}^{U} & V \subseteq U \\
      \id_{e} & V \not\subseteq U \\
      e \to \mathcal{F}(W) & W \subseteq U \subseteq V
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  where \(\res_{V, W}^{U}\) is the restriction in \(\PSh_{U}(\category{C})\) and \(e \to \mathcal{F}(W)\) the unique map from the \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e\).
  
  Note that this defines a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    j_{!}^{\pre}\colon \PSh_{U}(\category{C}) \to \PSh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

Before proving that this is indeed a \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{right-adjoint} to \(j^{-1}\) let's see how it interacts with \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-extension-by-zero-presheaf}
  Let \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  For any \hyperref[def:presheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued presheaf} \(\mathcal{F}\) the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk} at a point \(p \in X\) of the \hyperref[def:extension-by-zero-presheaf]{extension} \(j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F}\) is
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F} \right)_{p} = \begin{cases}
      e & p \notin U \\
      \mathcal{F}_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By \hyperref[def:stalk]{definition of stalk}
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F} \right)_{p} = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ U \in \Open(U)}} j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F}(V)
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore if \(p \notin U\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F} \right)_{p} = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ U \in \Open(U)}} j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ U \in \Open(U)}} e = e
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand if \(p \in U\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F} \right)_{p} = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ U \in \Open(U)}} j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F}(V) = \varinjlim_{\substack{V \ni p \\ U \in \Open(U)}} \mathcal{F}(V) = \mathcal{F}_{p} \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}

Using that \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness can be checked at the level of stalks} we get:
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:extension-by-zero-presheaf-exact}
  If \(\category{C}\) is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian} then the \hyperref[def:extension-by-zero-presheaf]{extension by zero functor of presheaves} \(j_{!}^{\pre}\colon \PSh_{U}(\category{C}) \to \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
\end{corollary}

Now we are in conditions to prove the \hyperref[def:adjoint-functor]{adjointness}.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:open-immersion-adjunction-presheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  Suppose that \(\category{C}\) has an \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e \in \category{C}\).

  There is a \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
    \PSh_{U}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, "{j_{!}^{\pre}}"{name=U}, bend left=25] & \PSh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, "{j_{\pre}^{-1}}"{name=D}, bend left=25]
    \arrow[phantom, from=U, to=D, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\mathcal{F} \in \PSh_{U}(\category{C})\) and \(\mathcal{G} \in \PSh_{X}(\category{C})\).
  We want to construct an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}
  \begin{equation*}
    \Phi\colon \Hom_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})}(j_{!}^{\pre}(\mathcal{F}), \mathcal{G}) \isoarrow \Hom_{\PSh_{U}(\category{C})}(\mathcal{F}, j_{\pre}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}))
  \end{equation*}

  Let \(\phi\colon j_{!}^{\pre}(\mathcal{F}) \to \mathcal{G}\) be a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} over \(X\).
  We want to define a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} \(\Phi(\phi)\colon \mathcal{F} \to j_{\pre}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\) over \(U\).
  Since \(j\) is an open map by \autoref{lemma:inverse-image-open-map} we have \(j_{\pre}^{-1} \mathcal{F}(V) = \mathcal{F}(V)\) for all open subsets \(V \subseteq X\).
  Therefore since \(\phi\) provides a component map \(\phi_{V}\colon j_{!}\mathcal{F}(V) \to \mathcal{G}(V)\) we can define
  \begin{equation*}
    \Phi(\phi)_{V} \coloneq \phi_{V}
  \end{equation*}
  Since \(\phi\) is a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf morphism} over \(X\) it commutes with the restriction maps for all open subsets of \(X\).
  In particular, it commutes with the restriction maps for all open subsets of \(U\) and thus \(\Phi(\phi)\) is a valid \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} over \(U\).

  Now let \(\psi\colon \mathcal{F} \to j_{\pre}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})\) be a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} over \(U\).
  We want to define a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} \(\Psi(\psi)\colon j_{!}^{\pre}(\mathcal{F}) \to \mathcal{G}\) over \(X\).
  For any open set \(W \subseteq X\) we define the component \(\Psi(\psi)_{W}\colon j_{!}^{\pre}\mathcal{F}(W) \to \mathcal{G}(W)\) componentwise as
  \begin{equation*}
    \Psi(\psi)_{W} \coloneq \begin{cases}
      \psi_{W} & W \subseteq U \\
      e \to \mathcal{G}(W) & W \not\subseteq U
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  Since this commutes with the restriction maps it is a valid \hyperref[def:presheaf]{morphism of presheaves} over \(X\).

  It only remains to see that \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are inverses.
  If \(V \subseteq U\) is an open subset then
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( \Phi \circ \psi \right)(\psi)_{V} = \Phi \left( \psi_{V} \right) = \psi_{V}
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand if \(W \subseteq X\) then we distinguish two cases.
  If \(W \subseteq U\) then
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( \Psi \circ \Phi \right)(\phi)_{W} = \Psi \left( \phi_{W} \right) = \phi_{W}
  \end{equation*}
  If \(W \not\subseteq U\) then both \(\left( \Psi \circ \Phi \right)(\phi)_{W}\) and \(\phi_{W}\) are maps from the \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e\) to \(\mathcal{G}(W)\) and therefore coincide.
\end{proof}

Let's extend this definition to the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} case using \hyperref[def:sheafification]{sheafification}.
\begin{definition}\label{def:extension-by-zero-sheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  Suppose that \(\category{C}\) has an \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e \in \category{C}\).

  The \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} \textbf{restriction} by \(e\) \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} is
  \begin{equation*}
    j_{!} \coloneq -^{\#} \circ j_{!}^{\pre} \circ \iota: \Sh_{U}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

Analogously to \autoref{lemma:stalk-extension-by-zero-presheaf} and \autoref{cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact} we see that \(j_{!}\) behaves as \(j_{!}^{\pre}\) in both sections and \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} and, in particular, we get that \(j_{!}\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-extension-by-zero-sheaf}
  Let \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  For any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}\) over \(U\) the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk} at a point \(p \in X\) of the \hyperref[def:extension-by-zero-sheaf]{extension} \(j_{!}\sheaf{F}\) is
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( j_{!}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} = \begin{cases}
      e & p \notin U \\
      \sheaf{F}_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:extension-by-zero-sheaf-exact}
  If \(\category{C}\) is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian} then the \hyperref[def:extension-by-zero-sheaf]{extension by zero functor of sheaves} \(j_{!}\colon \Sh_{U}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
\end{corollary}

Finally, we see that the \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction} is still present in the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} case.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:open-immersion-adjunction-sheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) be an open immersion.
  Suppose that \(\category{C}\) has an \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{initial object} \(e \in \category{C}\).

  There is a \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
    \Sh_{U}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, "{j_{!}}"{name=U}, bend left=25] & \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, "{j^{-1}}"{name=D}, bend left=25]
    \arrow[phantom, from=U, to=D, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  For all \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) and \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{U}(\category{C})\) we have
  \begin{align*}
    \Hom_{\Sh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( j_{!}(\sheaf{F}), \sheaf{G} \right) &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( \left( -^{\#} \circ j_{!}^{\pre} \circ \iota \right)(\sheaf{F}), \sheaf{G} \right) \\
                                                                            &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( \left( j_{!}^{\pre} \circ \iota \right)(\sheaf{F}), \iota(\sheaf{G}) \right) \\
                                                                            &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{U}(\category{C})} \left( \iota(\sheaf{F}), \left( j_{\pre}^{-1} \circ \iota \right)(\sheaf{G}) \right) \\
                                                                            &\cong \Hom_{\PSh_{U}(\category{C})} \left( \iota(\sheaf{F}), \left( \iota \circ j^{-1} \right)(\sheaf{G}) \right) \\
                                                                            &\cong \Hom_{\Sh_{U}(\category{C})} \left( \sheaf{F}, j^{-1}(\sheaf{G}) \right) \qedhere
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

We begin by noting the following fact about \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:stalk-closed-immersion}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf}.
  The \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} of \(i_{\ast}\sheaf{F}\) at a point \(p \in X\) is
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( i_{\ast}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} = \begin{cases}
      1 & p \notin Z \\
      \sheaf{F}_{p} & p \in Z
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  If \(p \notin Z\) then there exist arbitrarilly small open neighbourhoods \(U\) of \(p\) that do not intersect \(Z\).
  Therefore since \(\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}(i^{-1}(U)) = 1\), proving the first case.

  If \(p \in Z\) then any neighbourhood of \(p\) is of the form \(U \cap Z\) with \(U\) a neighbourhood of \(p\) in \(X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  If \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) is an open immersion and we consider \(i\colon X \setminus U \to X\) the closed immersion of the complement we have a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural isomorphism} \(i^{-1} \cong j_{\ast}\) since the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} are the same at all points.
\end{remark}

Taking as inspiration the above characterization, we define what the support of a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} is.
\begin{definition}\label{def:support-sheaf}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} over \(X\).
  The \textbf{support} of \(\sheaf{F}\) is
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{supp} \sheaf{F} \coloneq \{ p \in X \mid \sheaf{F}_{p} \neq 1 \}
  \end{equation*}
  If \(s \in \sheaf{F}(X)\) is a section then its support is
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{supp} s \coloneq \{ p \in X \mid s_{p} \neq 1 \text{ in } \sheaf{F}_{p} \}
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

This definition let's us describe the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image} more cleanly.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:closed-immersion-fully-faithful}
  The \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image sheaf functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    i_{\ast}\colon \Sh_{Z}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
  is \hyperref[def:functor-types]{fully faithfull}.
  Furthermore, its essential image consists of those \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves} whose \hyperref[def:support-sheaf]{support} is contained in \(Z\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{Z}(\category{C})\).
  Consider the map
  \begin{equation*}
    i^{-1}i_{\ast}\sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{F}
  \end{equation*}
  Since by \autoref{lemma:stalk-closed-immersion} this is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism} for all \(p \in Z\) by \autoref{prop:sheaf-isomorphism-determined-at-stalks} it is a \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}.
  Therefore \(i^{-1}i_{\ast} \cong \id_{\Sh_{Z}(\category{C})}\), which means that \(i_{\ast}\) is \hyperref[def:functor-types]{fully faithfull}.

  To see the essential image part, we are going to see that \(\sheaf{G} \in \operatorname{Im} i_{\ast}\) if and only if \(\sheaf{G}_{p} = 1\) for all \(p \in X \setminus Z\).
  Note that the direction \(\implies\) is direct from \autoref{lemma:stalk-closed-immersion}.
  To see \(\impliedby\), suppose that \(\sheaf{G}\) verifies \(\sheaf{G}_{p} = 1\) for all \(p \in X \setminus Z\).
  Then the map
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{G} \to i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{G}
  \end{equation*}
  is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism} on all \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} and thus by \autoref{prop:sheaf-isomorphism-determined-at-stalks} it is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}.
\end{proof}

With this in mind we can construct another \hyperref[def:adjoint-functor]{adjoint functor} \(i^{!}\) to \(i_{\ast}\), in this case with \(i_{\ast} \dashv i^{!}\).
\begin{definition}\label{def:extraordinary-inverse-image-sheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(Z \subseteq X\) a closed subset.
  For any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}\) over \(X\), the \textbf{extraordinary inverse image} is the \hyperref[def:types-of-morphisms-pre-sheaves]{subsheaf} \(i^{!}\sheaf{F}\) of \(\sheaf{F}\) defined as, for any open subset \(U \subseteq X\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left(i^{!}\sheaf{F}\right)(U) \coloneq \{ s \in \sheaf{F}(U) \mid \operatorname{supp} s \subseteq Z \cap U\}
  \end{equation*}
  Note that this is the largest \hyperref[def:types-of-morphisms-pre-sheaves]{subsheaf} of \(\sheaf{F}\) whose \hyperref[def:support-sheaf]{support} is contained in \(Z\).
  
  By \autoref{prop:closed-immersion-fully-faithful} we can view this as a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    i^{!}\colon \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{Z}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:closed-immersion-adjunction}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(i\colon Z \hookrightarrow X\) be a closed immersion.

  If \(\category{C}\) is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian}, there is a \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunction}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
    \Sh_{Z}(\category{C}) \arrow[r, "{i_{\ast}}"{name=U}, bend left=25] & \Sh_{X}(\category{C}) \arrow[l, "{i^{!}}"{name=D}, bend left=25]
    \arrow[phantom, from=U, to=D, "\perp"]
  \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  We want to show that, for any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} \(\sheaf{G} \in \Sh_{Z}(\category{C})\) and \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\), there is a natural bijection
  \begin{equation*}
    \Hom_{\Sh_{X}(\category{C})} \left( i_{\ast}\sheaf{G}, \sheaf{F} \right) \cong \Hom_{\Sh_{Z}(\category{C})} \left( \sheaf{G}, i^{!}\sheaf{F} \right)
  \end{equation*}

  Consider a morphism \(\phi\colon i_{\ast}\sheaf{G} \to \sheaf{F}\).
  We want to construct a morphism \(\psi\colon \sheaf{G} \to i^{!}\sheaf{F}\).

  For any open subset \(U \subseteq X\) we have a morphism in \(\category{C}\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \phi_{U}\colon \sheaf{G}(U \cap Z) \to \sheaf{F}(U)
  \end{equation*}
  Let \(W \subseteq Z\) be an open set and write \(W = U \cap Z\) for some \(U \subseteq X\) open.
  We want to show that the image of \(\phi_{U}\) lands on \(i^{!}\sheaf{F}(U)\).
  Let \(s \in \sheaf{G}(U \cap Z)\) and consider its restriction to the open set \(U \setminus Z\) of \(X\).
  By the commutativity of the restriction maps with \(\phi\) we get
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( \phi_{U}(s) \right)|_{U \setminus Z} = \phi_{U \setminus Z}(s|_{(U \setminus Z) \cap Z})
  \end{equation*}
  Since \((U \setminus Z) \cap Z = \varnothing\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( \phi_{U}(s) \right)|_{U \setminus Z} = \phi_{U \setminus Z}(s|_{(U \setminus Z) \cap Z}) = \phi_{U \setminus Z}(0) = 0
  \end{equation*}
  which means that
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{supp} (\phi_{U}(s)) \subseteq U \cap Z = W
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore since the image of \(\phi_{U}\) is contained in \(i^{!}\sheaf{F}(U)\) it naturally induces a map
  \begin{equation*}
    \psi_{W}\colon \sheaf{G}(W) \to i^{!}\sheaf{F}(W)
  \end{equation*}
  Since by construction these maps commute with the restrictions, these maps define a morphism \(\psi\colon \sheaf{G} \to i^{!}\sheaf{F}\).

  Conversely, suppose now that we have a morphism \(\psi\colon \sheaf{G} \to i^{!}\sheaf{F}\).
  We now want to construct a morphism \(\phi\colon i_{\ast}\sheaf{G} \to \sheaf{F}\).

  For any open subset \(V \subseteq X\) let \(H = V \cap Z\).
  We have a morphism in \(\category{C}\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \psi_{H}\colon \sheaf{G}(H) \to i^{!}\sheaf{F}(H)
  \end{equation*}
  By composing \(\psi_{H}\) with the inclussion \(i^{!}\sheaf{F}(H) \hookrightarrow \sheaf{F}(V)\) we obtain a map \(\phi_{V}\colon \sheaf{G}(V \cap Z) \to \sheaf{F}(V)\).
  Therefore since these maps commute with the restrictions they assemble into a morphism \(\phi\colon i_{\ast}\sheaf{G} \to \sheaf{F}\).
\end{proof}

Note that for closed immersions we have \hyperref[def:adjoint-functor]{adjunctions} \hyperref[thm:closed-immersion-adjunction]{\(i^{-1} \dashv i_{\ast} \dashv i^{!}\)}.
Therefore directly by \autoref{ex:adjoint-functors-and-exactness} we have:
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:closed-immersion-exact}
  If \(\category{C}\) is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian} then the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image sheaf functor}
  \begin{equation*}
    i_{\ast}\colon \Sh_{Z}(\category{C}) \to \Sh_{X}(\category{C})
  \end{equation*}
  is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{A canonical exact sequence}

We end this chapter by connecting open and closed immersions via a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence} that will become useful when we study \hyperref[def:relative-sheaf-cohomology]{relative sheaf cohomology} in \autoref{ch:cohomology-theories}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:SES-exactness}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space and \(U \subseteq X\) be an open subset.
  Consider the open and closed immersions \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) and \(i\colon X \setminus U \hookrightarrow X\).

  If \(\category{C}\) is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian} for all \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaves} \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) there exists a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Furthermore for any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{morphism of sheaves} \(\sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{G}\) we get a morphism of \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequences}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[d] \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[d] \arrow[r] & i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[d] \arrow[r] & 0 \\
      0 \arrow[r] & j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{G} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{G} \arrow[r] & i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{G} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  i.e: this construction is functorial.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Using the characterization of the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} at \autoref{lemma:stalk-inverse-image}, \autoref{lemma:inverse-image-stalk-open-map}, \autoref{lemma:stalk-extension-by-zero-presheaf} and \autoref{lemma:stalk-closed-immersion} we have
  \begin{align*}
    \left( j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} &= \begin{cases}
      0 & p \in X \setminus U \\
      \left( j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases} = \begin{cases}
      0 & p \in X \setminus U \\
      \sheaf{F}_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases} \\
    \left( i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} &= \begin{cases}
      0 & p \in X \setminus U \\
      \left( i^{-1}\sheaf{F} \right)_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases} = \begin{cases}
      0 & p \in X \setminus U \\
      \sheaf{F}_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases}
  \end{align*}
  Therefore for all \(p \in X\) the sequence
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \left(j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F}\right)_{p} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}_{p} \arrow[r] & \left(i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F}\right)_{p} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  is \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact} and therefore so is
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  The functoriallity comes from the naturality of the \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{adjunctions} \hyperref[thm:open-immersion-adjunction-sheaf]{\(j_{!} \dashv j^{-1} \dashv j_{\ast}\)} and \hyperref[thm:closed-immersion-adjunction]{\(i^{-1} \dashv i_{\ast} \dashv i^{!}\)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{notation}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{\(\category{C}\)-valued sheaf} over \(X\).
  If \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) is an open immersion we call \(j_{!}j^{-1}\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) the \textbf{extension} of \(\sheaf{F}\) by \(U\) and denote it by \(\sheaf{F}_{U}\).
  Analogously if \(i\colon Z \hookrightarrow X\) is a closed immersion we call \(i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F}\) the extension of \(\sheaf{F}\) by \(Z\) and denote it \(\sheaf{F}_{Z}\).

  Therefore with this notation \autoref{thm:SES-exactness} states that the following is an \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact sequence} for all open subsets \(U \subseteq X\)
   \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}_{U} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}_{X \setminus U} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{notation}
