\section{Sheaves} \label{sec:sheaves}

\todo[inline]{Write motivation on why sheaves capture more \textit{geometric} information}

We now turn our attention to a particular class of \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaves}.
Let's first tackle the case where the evaluation category is \(\Set\).

\begin{definition} \label{def:set-valued-sheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space.
  The \hyperref[def:category]{category} of \textbf{sheaves of sets} over \(X\) is the \hyperref[def:subcategory]{full subcategory} \(\Sh_{X}(\Set)\) of \(\PSh_{X}(\Set)\) whose objects verify, for every open set \(U \subseteq X\) and every open cover \(\{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) of \(U\)
  \begin{enumerate}[label=SH.\arabic*), ref=SH.\arabic*]
    \item\label{axiom:sheaf-identity} \textbf{Identity}: Given two sections \(s, t \in \sheaf{F}(U)\) such that for every \(i \in \mathcal{I} \quad s|_{U_{i}} = t|_{U_{i}}\) we have that \(s = t\).
    \item\label{axiom:sheaf-gluability} \textbf{Gluability}: Given a collection of sections \(\{s_{i} \mid s_{i} \in \sheaf{F}(U_{i})\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) that agree on pairwise intersections (\(\forall i, j \in \mathcal{I} \;\; s_{i}|_{U_{i} \cap U_{j}} = s_{j}|_{U_{i} \cap U_{j}}\)) there exists a section \(s \in \sheaf{F}(U)\) such that \(\forall i \in \mathcal{I} \;\; s|_{U_{i}} = s_{i}\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \hyperref[def:presheaf]{Presheaves} that only satisfy axiom \ref{axiom:sheaf-identity} are called \textbf{separated presheaves}.
\end{definition}

\begin{notation}
  From now on, to distinguish a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} from a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} we will denote the former by \(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{H}, \ldots\) and the latter by \(\sheaf{F}, \sheaf{G}, \sheaf{H}, \ldots\)
\end{notation}

Let's try to make \autoref{def:set-valued-sheaf} work on a more general evaluation category.
To do this, the first thing we need to do is to write axioms \ref{axiom:sheaf-identity} and \ref{axiom:sheaf-gluability} in terms of universal properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=SH.\arabic*)]
  \item Consider the morphism
        \begin{align*}
          e: \sheaf{F}(U) &\to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i}) \\
          s &\mapsto \{s|_{U_{i}}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}
        \end{align*}
        given by the restriction maps \(\res_{U, U_{i}}\).

        The condition \ref{axiom:sheaf-identity} is equivalent to \(e\) being injective, which (in \(\Set\)) means that \(e\) is a \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism}.
  \item Consider the morphisms
        \begin{align*}
          p, q: \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i}) \rightrightarrows \prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j})
        \end{align*}
        where \(p\) takes the section on \(U_{i}\) and sends it to the intersection and \(q\) takes the section on \(U_{j}\) and sends it to the intersection.

        The condition \ref{axiom:sheaf-gluability} is then equivalent to saying that \(\sheaf{F}\) is the \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer} of the diagram consisting of \(p\) and \(q\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore a \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} of sets \(\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} of sets if for every open set \(U\) and open cover \(\{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) of \(U\) the following diagram is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence}
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    \sheaf{F}(U) \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i}) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j})
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

Let's try to go now to a more general \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\category{C}\).
First of all, note that the \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} makes sense if \hyperref[def:categorical-product]{products} indexed by \(\mathcal{I}\) and \(\mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}\) exist in \(\category{C}\).
Furthermore, since our \hyperref[def:category]{categories} are \hyperref[def:category]{locally small categories} applying the \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(\Hom_{\category{C}}(-, -): \category{C} \times \category{C} \to \Set\) by the \hyperref[lemma:yoneda]{Yoneda lemma} we have that the diagram is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer} if and only if for every object \(C \in \category{C}\) the diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U)) \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U_{i})) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j}))
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer diagram} in \(\Set\).

\begin{definition}[Sheaf] \label{def:sheaf}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space.
  Let \(\category{C}\) be a \hyperref[def:category]{category} with \hyperref[def:categorical-product]{products} indexed by \(\mathcal{I}\) and \(\mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}\).

  The \hyperref[def:category]{category} of \(\category{C}\)-valued \textbf{sheaves} over \(X\) is the \hyperref[def:subcategory]{full subcategory} \(\Sh_{X}(\category{C})\) of the \hyperref[def:category]{category} of \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaves} over \(X\) whose objects verify that, for every open subset \(U \subseteq X\) and every open cover \(\{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) the diagram
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U)) \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U_{i})) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \Hom_{\category{C}}(C, \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j}))
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\Set\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  From now on, every time we work with \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves} we will tacitly assume that \(\category{C}\) has sufficient \hyperref[def:categorical-product]{products} so that \autoref{def:sheaf} makes sense.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark} \label{remark:empty-set-sheaf}
  Note that the empty set \(\varnothing \in \Open(X)\) is covered via the \textit{empty cover} \(\{U_{i}\}_{i \in \varnothing}\).
  Therefore for any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}\) we have that \(\sheaf{F}(\varnothing)\) is an element of the empty \hyperref[def:categorical-product]{product}, i.e: \(\sheaf{F}(\varnothing)\) is a \hyperref[def:initial-terminal-object]{terminal object} in \(\category{C}\).

  Therefore following \autoref{ex:one-point-presheaf} we have that, if \(\{\ast\}\) is the one-point topological space,
  \begin{align*}
    \Sh_{{\ast}}(\category{C}) \cong \category{C}
  \end{align*}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark} \label{remark:small-limits-sheaf}
  If \(\category{C}\) has \hyperref[def:categorical-limit]{small limits} then the \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \autoref{def:sheaf} can be written directly in \(\category{C}\).

  In this case, a \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} is a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} if for every open set \(U \subseteq X\) and open cover \(\{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) the following diagram
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \sheaf{F}(U) \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i}) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j})
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\category{C}\).
\end{remark}


\subsection{Sheaves of \(\Set\)-like categories} \label{ssec:concrete-category-sheaf}
Let's consider the case where our \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\category{C}\) comes with a \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(F: \category{C} \to \Set\).

Here, for every \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} \(\mathcal{F}\) we have an associated \textit{underlying \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf of sets}} \(F\mathcal{F}\).

We want to know in which \hyperref[def:category]{categories} we can check the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} conditions in the underlying \hyperref[def:presheaf]{presheaf} of sets.

We have the following diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
      \sheaf{F}(U) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i}) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] \arrow[d] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} \sheaf{F}(U_{i} \cap U_{j}) \arrow[d] \\
      (F\sheaf{F})(U) \arrow[r] & \displaystyle\prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} (F\sheaf{F})(U_{i}) \arrow[r, shift left] \arrow[r, shift right] & \displaystyle\prod_{(i, j) \in \mathcal{I} \times \mathcal{I}} (F\sheaf{F})(U_{i} \cap U_{j})
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
If we suppose that the top row is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\category{C}\) (\(\sheaf{F}\) is a \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}) then \(F\) needs to \hyperref[def:functor-and-limits]{preserve limits} to make the bottom row an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\Set\) (\(F\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:set-valued-sheaf]{sheaf of sets}).

Similarly, if we suppose that the bottom row is an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\Set\) (\(F\sheaf{F}\) is a \hyperref[def:set-valued-sheaf]{sheaf of sets}) then \(F\) needs to \hyperref[def:functor-and-limits]{reflect limits} to make the top row an \hyperref[def:equaliser]{equalizer sequence} in \(\category{C}\) (\(\sheaf{F}\) is a \(\category{C}\)-valued \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}).

Note that this does not happen in general for all \hyperref[def:concrete-category]{concrete categories}.
For example the \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(F: \Top \to \Set\) sending every topological space to its underlying set does not \hyperref[def:functor-and-limits]{reflect limits}.

Indeed, consider \(X = (\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T})\) the real plane with the usual product topology and \(Y = (\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T}_{d})\) the real plane with the discrete topology. Let \(Z = (\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T})\) be the real line with the usual topology.

Consider the diagram in \(\Top\)
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    & Y \arrow[dl, "p_{1}"'] \arrow[dr, "p_{2}"] & \\
    Z & & Z 
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
Note that this is not a \hyperref[def:categorical-limit]{limit} in \(\Top\).
Indeed the diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    & X \arrow[ddl, bend right, "\pi_{1}"'] \arrow[ddr, bend left, "\pi_{2}"] & \\
    & Y \arrow[dl, "p_{1}"'] \arrow[dr, "p_{2}"] & \\
    Z & & Z
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
cannot be completed since there does not exist a continuous map \(X \to Y\).

However, applying the forgetful functor to the diagram we get
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    & \mathbb{R}^{2} \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] & \\
    \mathbb{R} & & \mathbb{R} 
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
which is a \hyperref[def:categorical-limit]{limit} in \(\Set\) (it is the diagram of a \hyperref[def:categorical-product]{product}).

For other \hyperref[def:category]{categories} such as \(\mathsf{Ban}\) (the category of Banach spaces) the forgetful \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(F: \mathsf{Ban} \to \Set\) does not preserve limits.

Luckily \textit{algebraic categories} such as \(\Grp, \Ab, \Ring, \mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{K}}, \ldots\) do preserve and reflect limits.
Therefore in this categories we can \textit{forget} the structure when checking for \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} conditions.
