\chapter{Sheaf cohomology}

In this chapter, we establish the foundation for sheaf cohomology as the right derived functor of the \hyperref[def:global-sections-sheaf]{global sections functor}.
After verifying that the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{category of sheaves} of modules over a commutative ring \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{contains enough injective objects} to permit derived resolutions, we analyze how these cohomological invariants transform under domain restrictions and extensions.

\begin{notation}
  Given a ring \(R\), we denote the \hyperref[def:category]{category} of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of \(R\)-modules} over a topological space \(X\) by \(\Mod(\underline{R}_{X})\).
\end{notation}

In what follows, let \(\mathbf{k}\) be a fixed commutative ring.
Note that if we let \(\mathbf{k} = \mathbb{Z}\) since \(\Mod(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \Ab\) we have \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}) \cong \Sh_{X}(\Ab)\) and thus our work also applies to \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of abelian groups}.

\section{Injective sheaves and cohomology}


Note that \(\Mod(\mathbf{k})\) is an \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian category}.
Therefore by \autoref{thm:sheaf-category-is-abelian} \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) is also \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian}.

If we want to derive \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{left-exact functors} we need to show that \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:mod-ox-has-enough-injectives}
  The \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} over \(X\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  We will use the following claim
  \begin{quote}
    The \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\Mod(R)\) of modules over a ring \(R\) has enough injectives.
  \end{quote}
  For a proof, see \cite[Corollary VIII.6.4]{aluffiAlgebraChapter02009}.
  
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) and consider its \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk} \(\sheaf{F}_{p} \in \Mod(\mathbf{k})\) at a point \(p \in X\).

  Since \(\Mod(\mathbf{k})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives} we can choose an object \(I_{p}\) and a \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism}
  \begin{equation*}
    i_{p}\colon \sheaf{F}_{p} \monoarrow I_{p}
  \end{equation*}

  Consider the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{I} \coloneq \prod_{p \in X} \operatorname{sk}_{p} I_{p}
  \end{equation*}
  where \(\operatorname{sk}_{p}I_{p}\) is the \hyperref[def:skyscraper-sheaf]{skyscraper sheaf} supported at \(I_{p}\).
  Note that for all \(p \in X\) the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk} of \(\sheaf{I}\) at \(p\) is \(\sheaf{I}_{p} = I_{p}\).
  
  Therefore since \hyperref[prop:sheaf-isomorphism-determined-at-stalks]{sheaf morphisms are determined at the level of stalks} and the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk maps} \(i_{p}\colon \sheaf{F}_{p} \monoarrow I_{p}\) are \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphisms} the induced global map \(i\colon \sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{I}\) is a \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism}.

  Recall that by \autoref{cor:skyscraper-stalk-adjunction-sheaf} the \hyperref[def:skyscraper-sheaf]{skyscraper functor} is \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{right adjoint} to the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk functor}, which is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
  Therefore by \autoref{prop:adjoints-and-injectives} \(\sheaf{I}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective object} of \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\).
\end{proof}

This means that we can \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{derive} all \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{right-exact functors} from the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{category of sheaves of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules}.

Having seen this, we now can finally define sheaf cohomology.
Recall that by \autoref{cor:global-constant-adjunction-sheaf} \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{right adjoint}.
Therefore by \autoref{ex:adjoint-functors-and-exactness} \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{left exact}.
\begin{definition}[Sheaf Cohomology]\label{def:sheaf-cohomology}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} over a topological space \(X\).
  The \(i\)-th \textbf{cohomology} of \(X\) with coefficients in \(\sheaf{F}\) is the \(i\)-th \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{right derived functor} of the \hyperref[def:global-sections-sheaf]{global sections functor}:
  \begin{equation*}
    \cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{F}) \coloneq \operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma(X, -)(\sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
  By abuse of notation we will write this \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{derived functor} as
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}) \coloneq \operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma(X, -)(\sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}\label{ex:cohomology-over-point}
  Let \(\{\ast\}\) be the one point topological space.
  For all \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{\{\ast\}})\) and \(n \neq 0\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \cohomology{n}(\{\ast\}, \sheaf{F}) \cong 0
  \end{equation*}
  Indeed by definition \(\cohomology{n}( \{\ast\}, \sheaf{F} ) = \operatorname{R}^{n} \Gamma(\{\ast\}, \sheaf{F})\) and since \(\Gamma(\{\ast\}, \sheaf{F}) = \sheaf{F}(\{\ast\}) = \sheaf{F}_{\ast}\) by the \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{exactness of the stalk functor} for all \(n \neq 0\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \cohomology{n} (\{\ast\}, \sheaf{F}) = \operatorname{R}^{n} \sheaf{F}_{\ast} = 0 \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{example}

\subsection{Acyclic resolutions}

In \autoref{lemma:mod-ox-has-enough-injectives} we have seen that, even though they exist, \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injectives sheaves} are complicated objects.
Recall that \hyperref[thm:right-derived-functor-using-acyclics]{cohomology can be computed using \(\Gamma(X, -)\)-acyclic resolutions}.

In this section we introduce a class of \hyperref[def:F-acyclic-object]{\(\Gamma(X, -)\)-acyclic} objects that will help us later.

\begin{definition}[Flasque sheaf]\label{def:flasque-sheaf}
  A \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F}\) is \textbf{flasque} (or \textbf{flabby}) if for any inclusion of open subsets \(V \subseteq U\) the restriction map \(\res_{U, V}\colon \sheaf{F}(U) \to \sheaf{F}(V)\) is surjective.
\end{definition}

A particular case of \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque sheaves} are the \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} ones.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:injective-sheaves-are-flasque}
  \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{Injective} \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves} are \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(U' \subseteq U \subseteq X\) be open subsets and \(\sheaf{I} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) be an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}.
  We want to see that the map \(\sheaf{I}(U) \to \sheaf{I}(U')\) is surjective.

  Consider the open immersions \(j\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) and \(j'\colon U' \hookrightarrow X\).
  Since \hyperref[thm:open-immersion-adjunction-sheaf]{\(j_{!}\) is left adjoint to the restriction} for any \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}\) on \(U\), we have an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}:
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(j_{!} \sheaf{F}, \sheaf{I}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U})}(\sheaf{F}, \sheaf{I}|_{U})
  \end{equation*}
  
  In particular considering \(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U}\), we obtain:
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(j_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U}, \sheaf{I}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U})}(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U}, \sheaf{I}|_{U}) \cong \sheaf{I}(U)
  \end{equation*}
  Analogously for \(U'\) we have \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(j'_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U'}, \sheaf{I}) \cong \sheaf{I}(U')\).

  The inclusion \(U' \subseteq U\) induces a natural \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism} of sheaves on \(X\):
  \begin{equation*}
    j'_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U'} \monoarrow j_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U}
  \end{equation*}
  
  Because \(\sheaf{I}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective object}, the \hyperref[ex:hom-functor]{covariant functor \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(-, \sheaf{I})\)} is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}, meaning it transforms \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphisms} into \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{epimorphisms}. Applying this \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} to our inclusion yields a surjective map:
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(j_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U}, \sheaf{I}) \epiarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})}(j'_{!} \underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U'}, \sheaf{I})
  \end{equation*}
  
  Under the \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphisms} established above, this is precisely the restriction map \(\sheaf{I}(U) \to \sheaf{I}(U')\). Thus, the restriction map is surjective, meaning \(\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.
\end{proof}

Therefore we can always find a \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque resolution} of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flasque-are-acyclic}
  \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{Flasque sheaves} are \hyperref[def:F-acyclic-object]{\(\Gamma(X, -)\)-acyclic}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque sheaf}.
  Since \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives} we have a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{I} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{G} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  where \(\sheaf{I}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective object}.
  We will first show that, for any open subset \(U \subseteq X\), the map \(\sheaf{I}(U) \to \sheaf{G}(U)\) is surjective.
  
  Let \(s \in \sheaf{G}(U)\).
  Let \(V, W \subseteq U\) be two open subsets and \(t_{V} \in \sheaf{I}(V), t_{W} \in \sheaf{I}(W)\) be two sections lifting \(s\) to \(\sheaf{I}\).
  Consider
  \begin{equation*}
    t_{V}|_{V \cap W} - t_{W}|_{V \cap W} \in \sheaf{F}(V \cap W)
  \end{equation*}
  Since \(\sheaf{F}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque} there exists a section \(x_{V} \in \sheaf{F}(V)\) such that \(x_{V}|_{V \cap W} = t_{V} - t_{W}\).
  Therefore if \(t'_{V} = t_{V} - x_{V}\) we have \(t'_{V}|_{V \cap W} = t_{W}|_{V \cap W}\), i.e: there exists a section \(t \in \sheaf{I}(V \cup W)\) such that \(t|_{V} = t'|_{V}\) and \(t'|_{W} = t|_{W}\).
  Clearly \(t\) is sent to \(s|_{V \cup W}\).

  Consider now a pair \((W, t)\) which is maximal (for the obvious order relation), where \(W\) is an open subset of \(U\) and \(t\) is a section of \(\sheaf{I}\) lifting \(s\) to \(W\).
  Since \(s\) lifts locally on \(\sheaf{I}\), if \(W \neq U\) we could choose an open neighborhood in \(U \setminus W\) with a local lift and strictly extend \(W\) using the gluing procedure above, contradicting maximality. Thus, \(W = U\) and \(\sheaf{I}(U) \to \sheaf{G}(U)\) is surjective.
  
  The \hyperref[thm:LES-derived-functor]{long exact sequence in cohomology} of our sequence is
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}(X) \arrow[r] & \sheaf{I}(X) \arrow[r, two heads] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=X}] & \sheaf{G}(X) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (X) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{I}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=Y}] &  \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{G}) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (Y) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{I}) \arrow[r] & \cdots
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Note that since \(\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} we have \(\cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{I}) = 0\) for all \(i > 0\).
  In particular using that \(\sheaf{I}(X) \epiarrow \sheaf{G}(X)\) is surjective we have \(\cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}) = 0\).

  For \(k \geq 2\) we get \(\cohomology{k}(X, \sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{k-1}(X, \sheaf{G})\).
  Note that since \hyperref[lemma:injective-sheaves-are-flasque]{injective sheaves are flasque} \(\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.
  Therefore the surjectivity of the maps \(\sheaf{I}(U) \epiarrow \sheaf{G}(U)\) for all open \(U\) implies that \(\sheaf{G}\) is also flasque.
  Indeed this follows directly from the diagram
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \sheaf{I}(U) \arrow[r, two heads] \arrow[d, two heads, "\res_{U, V}"'] & \sheaf{G}(U) \arrow[d] \\
      \sheaf{I}(V) \arrow[r, two heads] & \sheaf{G}(V)
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  By induction over \(k\) it follows that
  \begin{equation*}
    \forall k > 0 \quad \cohomology{k}(X, \sheaf{F}) = 0 \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}


Using \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque sheaves} we can construct a canonical (and functorial) resolution of a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} to compute its \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology}, the so called Godement resolution.
We only give here a sketch of its construction and refer the reader to \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FKR}{Section 0FKR}]{stacks-project} for more details.
\begin{construction}[Godement resolution]\label{def:godement-resolution}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules}.
  The \textbf{Godement resolution} of \(\sheaf{F}\) is constructed by considering the inclusion of \(\sheaf{F}\) into the \textit{Godement sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}\)
  \begin{equation*}
    U \mapsto \bigoplus_{p \in U} \sheaf{F}_{p} \eqcolon \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(U)
  \end{equation*}
  Note that \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.

  We then inject the quotient \(\quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}}\) into \(\left( \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}} \right)_{\operatorname{God}}\) and so on.
\end{construction}

\begin{example}
  Let's try to compute \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} using the \hyperref[def:godement-resolution]{Godement resolution}.

  Let \(X = \{p, q\}\) with the \textit{Sierpiński} topology \(\mathcal{T} = \{\varnothing, \{p\}, X\}\).
  Consider the \hyperref[def:constant-sheaf]{constant sheaf} supported at the integers \(\sheaf{F} = \underline{\mathbb{Z}}\).

  To compute the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology} of \(\sheaf{F}\), we are going to construct the complex of global sections:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}) \arrow[r, "\d^{0}"] & \Gamma\left(X, \left(\quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}} \right)_{\operatorname{God}}\right) \arrow[r, "\d^{1}"] & \cdots
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  Note that the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} of \(\sheaf{F}\) are:
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{F}_{p} = \sheaf{F}(\{p\}) = \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \sheaf{F}_{q} = \sheaf{F}(X) = \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}

  Consider the Godement sheaf associated to \(\sheaf{F}\):
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(U) = \bigoplus_{x \in U} \sheaf{F}_{x}.
  \end{equation*}
  We have that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}) = \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(X) = \sheaf{F}_{p} \oplus \sheaf{F}_{q} = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}
  This means that we have a map \(\d^{-1}\) as follows:
  \begin{equation*}
    \d^{-1}: \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F})}_{\mathbb{Z}} \to \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}})}_{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}}, \quad \d^{-1}(k) = (k, k).
  \end{equation*}
  Let \(\sheaf{Q}^1 = \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}}\) be the \hyperref[def:categorical-cokernel]{cokernel} sheaf.
  If we compute its \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}, we have that:
  \begin{align*}
    (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{p} &= \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}, p}}{\sheaf{F}_{p}} = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0, \\
    (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{q} &= \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}, q}}{\sheaf{F}_{q}} = \quot{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}}{\langle(k, k)\rangle} \cong \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{align*}
  Therefore, the \hyperref[def:global-sections-sheaf]{global sections} of the Godement sheaf of \(\sheaf{Q}^{1}\) are:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma\left(X, (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\right) = (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{p} \oplus (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{q} = 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}
  This produces a differential map:
  \begin{equation*}
    \d^{0}: \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}})}_{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}} \to \underbrace{\Gamma\left(X, (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\right)}_{\mathbb{Z}}, \quad \d^{0}(m, n) = m - n.
  \end{equation*}
  Let \(\sheaf{Q}^2\) be the \hyperref[def:categorical-cokernel]{cokernel} of the map \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}} \to (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\).
  If we compute its \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks}, we have:
  \begin{align*}
    (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{p} &= \quot{0}{0} = 0, \\
    (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{q} &= \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0.
  \end{align*}
  Therefore, we have that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma \left( X, (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{\operatorname{God}} \right) = 0 \oplus 0 = 0.
  \end{equation*}
  This means that our resolution of global sections is:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
      0 \arrow[r] & \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r, "d^{0}", "{(m, n) \mapsto m - n}"'] & \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Therefore, the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology groups} of \(X\) with coefficients in \(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\) are:
  \begin{align*}
    \cohomology{0}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= \ker(d^{0}) = \{(m, n) \in \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \mid m - n = 0\} \cong \mathbb{Z}, \\
    \cohomology{1}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= \operatorname{coker}(d^{0}) = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\operatorname{im}(d^{0})} = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0, \\
    \cohomology{i}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= 0 \quad \forall i > 1.
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

This example shows that computing \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} using the \hyperref[def:godement-resolution]{Godement resolution} is an almost imposible task in general.
In the following chapters we will see how we can solve this problem.

\section{Cohomology and subsets}
We are going to see how \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} behaves when restricting and extending sheaves to subsets of \(X\).
Before we do so, we need to understand how \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} behave under \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct} and \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse images}.
\begin{notation}
  If \(Y \subseteq X\) is a subset of \(X\) and \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) recall that we set \(\Gamma(Y, \sheaf{F}) = \Gamma(Y, \sheaf{F}|_{Y})\), where \(\sheaf{F}|_{Y} = i_{Y}^{-1}(\sheaf{F})\) and \(i_{Y}\colon Y \hookrightarrow X\) is the inclusion.
  We set now
  \begin{equation*}
    \cohomology{i}(Y, \sheaf{F}) \coloneq \operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma(Y, -)(\sheaf{F}) = \operatorname{R}^{i}\left( a_{Y, \ast} \circ i_{Y}^{-1} \right) (\sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
  where \(a_{Y}\colon Y \to \{\ast\}\).
\end{notation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:injective-sheaves-and-maps}
  Let \(\sheaf{I} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) be an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules}.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If \(f\colon X \to Y\) is a continuous map then \(f_{\ast}\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} in \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{Y})\).
    \item If \(j_{U}\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) is an open immersion then \(\sheaf{I}|_{U} = j_{U}^{-1}\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} in \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{U})\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}~
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Since \hyperref[cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact]{\(f^{-1}\) is exact}  and \(\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective} the \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(\Hom_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbb{k}}_{X})}(f^{-1}(-), \sheaf{I})\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
          Using the \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-sheaf]{adjunction \(f^{-1} \dashv f_{\ast}\)} we have
          \begin{equation*}
            \Hom_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbb{k}}_{X})}(f^{-1}(-), \sheaf{I}) \cong \Hom_{\Mod(\underline{\mathbb{k}}_{Y})}(-, f_{\ast}\sheaf{I})
          \end{equation*}
          and thus by \hyperref[prop:injective-characterization-hom]{the characterization of injective objects} we have that \(f_{\ast}\sheaf{I}\) is \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective}.
    \item Analogous to the previous item using the \hyperref[cor:extension-by-zero-sheaf-exact]{exactness of \(j_{U, !}\)} and the \hyperref[thm:open-immersion-adjunction-sheaf]{adjunction \(j_{U, !} \dashv j_{U}^{-1}\)}. \qedhere
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cohomology-subsets}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space, \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} and \(i \geq 0\).
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If \(U \subseteq X\) is open we have \(\cohomology{i}(U, \sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{i}(U, \sheaf{F}|_{U})\).
    \item If \(Z \subseteq X\) is closed we have \(\cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z}) \cong \cohomology{i}(Z, \sheaf{F}|_{Z})\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(i_{Y}\colon Y \hookrightarrow X\) be the inclusion of \(Y \subseteq X\) and \(a_{Y}\colon Y \to \{\ast\}\).
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item We have
          \begin{equation*}
            \cohomology{i}(U, \sheaf{F}) \cong \operatorname{R}^{i}(a_{U, \ast} \circ i_{U}^{-1})(\sheaf{F}) \cong \left(\operatorname{R}^{i} a_{U, \ast} \right)(i_{U}^{-1}\sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{i}(U, \sheaf{F}|_{U})
          \end{equation*}
          where the second \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism} comes from \autoref{thm:derived-functor-composition} since \hyperref[cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact]{\(i_{U}^{-1}\) is exact} and \hyperref[lemma:injective-sheaves-and-maps]{sends injectives to injectives}.
    \item We have
          \begin{equation*}
            \cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z}) \cong \left( \operatorname{R}^{i}a_{X, \ast} \right)(( i_{Z, \ast} \circ i_{Z}^{-1}) (\sheaf{F})) \cong \left( \operatorname{R}^{i} (a_{X} \circ i_{Z})_{\ast} \right) (i_{Z}^{-1}(\sheaf{F})) \cong \cohomology{i}(Z, \sheaf{F}|_{Z})
          \end{equation*}
          where the second \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism} comes from \autoref{thm:derived-functor-composition} since \hyperref[cor:closed-immersion-exact]{\(i_{Z, \ast}\) is exact} and \hyperref[lemma:injective-sheaves-and-maps]{sends injectives to injectives}. \qedhere
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

We also have a stronger results regarding Hausdorff and paracompact spaces.

Before stating it, let's recall some basic topological facts about paracompact spaces.
A paracompact space \(X\) is a space such that for each open covering \(\mathcal{U} = \{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) of \(X\) there exists an open covering \(\mathcal{V} = \{V_{j}\}_{j \in \mathcal{J}}\) finer than \(\mathcal{U}\) (i.e: for all \(j \in \mathcal{J}\) there exists \(i \in \mathcal{I}\) such that \(V_{j} \subseteq U_{i}\)) and locally finite.
Furthermore if \(X\) is Hausdorff and \(\mathcal{U} = \{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) is an open and locally finite cover of \(X\) then there exists an open covering \(\mathcal{V} = \{V_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) such that \(\operatorname{ad}(V_{i}) \subset U_{i}\) for all \(i \in \mathcal{I}\).
Recall also that closed subspaces of a paracompact space are also paracompact.
For a proof of these results see \cite[Lemma 41.6]{munkresTopology2014} and \cite[Theorem 41.2]{munkresTopology2014} respectively.

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Proposition 2.5.1]{kashiwaraCategoriesSheaves2006}}]\label{lemma:cohomology-compact-subsets}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space, \(Z \subseteq X\) a subspace and \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\).
  Consider the \hyperref[def:inverse-limit]{canonical morphism} in \(\Mod(\mathbf{k})\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \psi\colon \varinjlim_{\substack{U \supset Z \\ U \in \Open(X)}} \Gamma(U, \sheaf{F}) \to \Gamma(Z, \sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
  Then \(\psi\) is injective.
  Furthermore if \(X\) is Hausdorff (resp. Hausdorff and paracompact) and \(Z\) is compact (resp. closed) then \(\psi\) is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}.
  
  Under these conditions for all \(i \geq 0\) there is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}
  \begin{equation*}
    \varinjlim_{\substack{U \supset Z \\ U \in \Open(X)}}  \cohomology{i}(U, \sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{i}(Z, \sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  For the injectivity note that if \(s \in \Gamma(U, \sheaf{F})\) is zero in \(\Gamma(Z, \sheaf{F})\) then for all \(p \in Z\) germ \(s_{p} = 0\).
  Therefore \(s\) is zero in an open neighbourhood of \(Z\) and the map is injective.

  For the surjectivity consider \(s \in \Gamma(Z, \sheaf{F})\).
  Then there exists a family of open subsets \(\mathcal{U} = \{U_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) with \(Z \subset \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} U_{i}\) and \(s_{i} \in \Gamma(U_{i}, \sheaf{F})\) such that \(s|_{U_{i} \cap Z} = s_{i}|_{U_{i} \cap Z}\) for all \(i \in \mathcal{I}\).

  If \(Z\) is compact we may assume that \(\mathcal{I}\) is finite and if \(X\) is paracompact we may assume that \(\mathcal{U}\) is a locally finite covering of \(X\).
  In either case we can find a family of open subsets \(\mathcal{V} = \{V_{i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}\) such that \(\mathcal{V}\) is locally finite, \(Z \subset \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} V_{i}\) and \(\operatorname{ad}(V_{i}) \subset U_{i}\) for all \(i \in \mathcal{I}\).
  For \(p \in X\) define \(\mathcal{I}(p) \coloneq \{i \in \mathcal{I} \mid p \in \operatorname{ad}(V_{i})\}\) and let
  \begin{equation*}
    W \coloneq \left\{ p \in \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} V_{i} \mid (s_{i})_{p} = (s_{j})_{p} \text{ for any } i, j \in \mathcal{I}(p) \right\}
  \end{equation*}
  Note that \(\mathcal{I}(p)\) is a finite set and each \(p \in X\) has a neighbourhood \(W_{p}\) such that \(\mathcal{I}(y) \subseteq \mathcal{I}(p)\) for all \(y \in W_{p}\).
  Therefore \(W\) is open and contains \(Z\) by construction.

  Finally, since \(s_{i}|_{W \cap V_{i} \cap V_{j}} = s_{j}|_{W \cap V_{i} \cap V_{j}}\) there exists \(\widetilde{s} \in \Gamma(W, \sheaf{F})\) such that \(\widetilde{s}|_{W \cap V_{i}} = s_{i}|_{W \cap V_{i}}\) and thus \(\psi(\widetilde{s}) = s\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mayer-Vietoris}

The functorial behavior of restriction and extension established above suggests that we can compute the global cohomology of a space by splitting it into simpler, overlapping sub-domains.
In classical algebraic topology, the standard technique for computing invariants via open or closed decompositions is the Mayer-Vietoris sequence.

It is worth noting that the following results work \textit{mutatus mutandis} replacing \(\Gamma(X, -)\) with any other \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image functor} \(f_{\ast}\) for any continuous map \(f\colon X \to Y\).

First we study the splitting via closed subsets.
\begin{theorem}[Closed Mayer-Vietoris]\label{thm:mayer-vietoris-closed}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space.
  Suppose that \(X = Z \cup W\) for some closed sets \(Z, W\).
  Then for every \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) there exists a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{long exact sequence} in \hyperref[def:cohomology]{cohomology}:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \cohomology{0}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{0}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z}) \oplus \cohomology{0}(X, \sheaf{F}_{W}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=X}] & \cohomology{0}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z \cap W}) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (X) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z}) \oplus \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}_{W}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=Y}] &  \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z \cap W}) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (Y) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{F}_{Z}) \oplus \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{F}_{W}) \arrow[r] & \cdots
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  We are going to see that the sequence of \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaves}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}_{Z} \oplus \sheaf{F}_{W} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}_{Z \cap W} \arrow[r] & 0 
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  is \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact} by \hyperref[cor:exactness-at-stalks]{checking that it is exact at the level of stalks}.
  
  Recall that if \(i\colon U \hookrightarrow X\) is a closed immersion by \autoref{lemma:stalk-inverse-image} and \autoref{lemma:stalk-closed-immersion} the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalk} of \(\sheaf{F}_{U} = i_{\ast}i^{-1}\sheaf{F}\) at a point \(p \in X\) is
  \begin{align*}
    \left( \sheaf{F}_{U} \right)_{p} = \begin{cases}
      0 & p \in X \setminus U \\
      \sheaf{F}_{p} & p \in U
    \end{cases}
  \end{align*}
  Three easy checks for \(p\) belonging to \(X \setminus Z, X \setminus W\) and \(Z \cap W\) show that the induced sequence in the \hyperref[def:stalk]{stalks} is \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact}.

  Therefore applying the \hyperref[thm:LES-derived-functor]{long exact sequence} associated to the \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{right derived functor} \(\operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma\) we get the result.
\end{proof}

The case for open splittings is a little bit more difficult.
For our proof to be valid when replacing \(\Gamma\) with another \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image functor} we need to use that \hyperref[lemma:injective-sheaves-are-flasque]{injective sheaves are flasque}.
\begin{theorem}[Open Mayer-Vietoris]\label{thm:mayer-vietoris-open}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space.
  Suppose that \(X = U \cup V\) for some open sets \(U, V\).
  Then for every \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf of \(\mathbf{k}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{X})\) there exists a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{long exact sequence} in \hyperref[def:cohomology]{cohomology}:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \cohomology{0}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{0}(U, \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{0}(V, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=X}] & \cohomology{0}(U \cap V, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (X) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{1}(U, \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{1}(V, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, phantom, ""{coordinate, name=Y}] &  \cohomology{1}(U \cap V, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[dll, rounded corners, to path={
        -- ([xshift=2ex]\tikztostart.east)
        |- (Y) [near end]\tikztonodes
        -| ([xshift=-2ex]\tikztotarget.west)
        -- (\tikztotarget)}] \\
      & \cohomology{2}(X, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{2}(U, \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{2}(V, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cdots
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\) be an \hyperref[def:resolution]{injective resolution} of \(\sheaf{F}\).
  We are going to see that the following is a \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{short exact sequence} of \hyperref[def:complex]{complexes}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2em]
      0 \arrow[r] & \Gamma(X, \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}) \arrow[r, "\alpha"] & \Gamma(U, \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}|_{U}) \oplus \Gamma(V, \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}|_{V}) \arrow[r, "\beta"] & \Gamma(U \cap V, \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}|_{U \cap V}) \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  where \(\alpha(s) \coloneq (s|_{U}, s|_{V})\) and \(\beta(s, t) \coloneq s|_{U \cap V} - t|_{U \cap V}\).
  
  For each \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exactness} at \(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{I}^{n})\) and the middle term follow directly from the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf axioms} on the open cover \(X = U \cup V\).
  To see \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exactness} at \(\Gamma(U \cap V, \sheaf{I}^{n}|_{U \cap V})\) recall that \hyperref[lemma:injective-sheaves-are-flasque]{injective sheaves are flasque}.
  Therefore since \(U \cap V \subseteq U\) for any \(r \in \Gamma(U \cap V, \sheaf{I}^{n}|_{U \cap V}) \cong \sheaf{I}^{n}|_{U \cap V}(U \cap V)\) there exists \(s \in \Gamma(U, \sheaf{I}^{n}|_{U}) \cong \sheaf{I}^{n}|_{U}(U)\) such that \(s|_{U \cap V} = r\) and thus \(\beta(s, 0) = r\) making \(\beta\) surjective.

  Therefore taking the associated \hyperref[thm:LES-in-cohomology]{long exact sequence in cohomology} and using \autoref{prop:cohomology-subsets} we have the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  One can generalize the splitting to more than two open (or closed) sets, obtaining what is called \v{C}ech cohomology.
  We will not need this generalization though, so we refer the interested reader to \cite[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01ED}{Section 01ED}]{stacks-project} for more details.
\end{remark}
