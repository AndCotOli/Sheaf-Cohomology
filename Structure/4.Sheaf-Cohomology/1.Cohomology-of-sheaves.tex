\chapter{Cohomology of sheaves}

Let \((X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\) be a \hyperref[def:ringed-spaced]{ringed space}.

We want to define the cohomology of \(X\) with coefficients in a \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} as the \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{right derived functor} of the \hyperref[def:global-section-sheaf]{global sections functor}
\begin{align*}
  \Gamma(X, -): \Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X}) \to \Mod(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X}))
\end{align*}
To do this, we need to check the following:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X})\) and \(\Mod(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X}))\) are \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian categories}.
  \item The \hyperref[def:functor]{functor} \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{left exact}.
  \item \(\Mod(\sheaf{O})_{X}\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives}.
\end{enumerate}

In \autoref{thm:mod-ox-is-abelian} we have proved that the category of \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaves of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} is \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian}.
Furthermore, since \(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\) is a ring, \(\Mod(X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\) is also \hyperref[def:abelian-category]{abelian}.

Recall that by (--) \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{right adjoint} to \(- \otimes_{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X})} \sheaf{O}_{X}\).
Therefore by \autoref{thm:adjointness-and-exactness} \(\Gamma(X, -)\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{left exact}.

It only remains to show that \(\Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives}.
\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:mod-ox-has-enough-injectives}
  Let \((X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\) be a \hyperref[def:ringed-space]{ringed space}.
  The \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X})\) of \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaves of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} over \(X\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  We will use the following claim
  \begin{quote}
    The \hyperref[def:category]{category} \(\Mod(R)\) of modules over a ring \(R\) has enough injectives.
  \end{quote}
  For a proof, see \cite[Corollary VIII.6.4]{aluffiAlgebraChapter02009}.
  
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be an \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-module.
  Let \(\sheaf{F}_{p} \in \Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X, p})\) be that \hyperref[def:stalk-sheaf]{stalk} of \(\sheaf{F}\) at a point \(p \in X\).

  Since \(\Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X, p})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives} we can choose an object \(I_{p}\) and a \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism}
  \begin{equation*}
    i_{p}: \sheaf{F}_{p} \monoarrow I_{p}
  \end{equation*}

  Consider the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf}
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{I} \coloneq \bigoplus_{p \in X} \iota_{p, \ast} \underline{I_{p}}
  \end{equation*}
  where \(\iota_{p, \ast}\underline{I_{p}}\) is the \hyperref[def:skyscraper-sheaf]{skyscraper sheaf}.
  More explicitly, for every open set \(U \subseteq X\) the \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} \(\sheaf{I}\) is defined as
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{I}(U) = \bigoplus_{p \in U} I_{p}
  \end{equation*}

  Recall that by \autoref{cor:stalk-skyscraper-adjunction-presheaf} the \hyperref[def:skyscraper-sheaf]{skyscraper functor} is \hyperref[def:adjoint-functors]{left-adjoint} to the \hyperref[def:stalk-sheaf]{stalk functor}, which is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact}.
  Therefore by \autoref{prop:left-adjoints-and-injectives} \(\sheaf{I}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective object}.

  Furthermore, there exists a \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{monomorphism} \(\phi: \sheaf{F} \to \sheaf{I}\) defined as, for any open set \(U \subseteq X\)
  \begin{align*}
    \phi(U):\,& \sheaf{F}(U) \to \sheaf{I}(U) \\
           & s \mapsto \bigoplus_{p \in U} j_{p}(s_{p})
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}

Having seen this, we now can finally define sheaf cohomology.
\begin{definition}[Sheaf Cohomology] \label{def:sheaf-cohomology}
  Let \((X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\) be a \hyperref[def:ringed-space]{ringed space}.
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} over a topological space \(X\).
  The \(i\)-th \textbf{cohomology} of \(X\) with coefficients in \(\sheaf{F}\) as the \(i\)-th \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{right derived functor} of the \hyperref[def:global-section-sheaf]{global sections functor}:
  \begin{equation*}
    \cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{F}) \coloneq R^{i}\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F})
  \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

Therefore, given a \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F}\) to compute its \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology} we \textit{only} need to construct an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-resolution]{injective} (or \hyperref[def:acyclic-object]{\(\Gamma(X, -)\) acyclic} by \autoref{thm:dimension-shifting}) resolution of \(\sheaf{F}\)
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}^{(0)} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F}^{(1)} \arrow[r] & \cdots
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
and compute the \hyperref[def:cohomology]{cohomology} of the \(\Gamma(X, \sheaf{O}_{X})\)-modules
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    0 \arrow[r] & \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}^{(0)}) \arrow[r] & \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}^{(1)}) \arrow[r] & \cdots
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

Since \hyperref[def:injective-projective-resolution]{injective resolutions} are usually hard to find (note how \autoref{lemma:mod-ox-has-enough-injectives} doesn't give an \textit{easy} construction) we will mostly work with \hyperref[def:acyclic-object]{\(\Gamma(X, -)\) acyclic} resolutions.

\section{The Godement resolution}

\begin{definition}[Flasque sheaf] \label{def:flasque-sheaf}
  A \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} \(\sheaf{F}\) is \textbf{flasque} (or \textbf{flabby}) if for any inclussion of open subsets \(V \subseteq U\) the restriction map \(\res_{U, V}: \sheaf{F}(U) \to \sheaf{F}(V)\) is surjective.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
  \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{Flasque sheaves} are \hyperref[def:acyclic-object]{\(\Gamma(X, -)\) acyclic}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque sheaf}.
  Since \(\Mod(\sheaf{O}_{X})\) has \hyperref[def:enough-injectives-projectives]{enough injectives} we have a \hyperref[def:short-exact-sequence]{short exact sequence}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \sheaf{F} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{I} \arrow[r] & \sheaf{G} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  where \(\sheaf{I}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective object}.

  We will first show that, for any open subset \(U \subseteq X\), the map \(\sheaf{I}(U) \epiarrow \sheaf{G}(U)\) is surjective.
  Let \(s \in \sheaf{G}(U)\).
  Let \(V, W \subseteq U\) be two open subsets and \(\tau_{V} \in \sheaf{I}(V), \tau_{W} \in \sheaf{I}(W)\) be two sections lifting \(s\) to \(\sheaf{I}\).
  Consider
  \begin{equation*}
    \tau_{V}|_{V \cap U} - \tau_{W}|_{V \cap W} \in \sheaf{F}(V \cap W)
  \end{equation*}
  Since \(\sheaf{F}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque} there exists a section \(\chi_{V} \in \sheaf{F}(V)\) such that \(\chi_{V}|_{V \cap W} = \tau_{V} - \tau_{W}\).
  Therefore if \(\tau'_{V} = \tau_{V} - \chi_{V}\) we have \(\tau'_{V}|_{V \cap W} = \tau_{W}|_{V \cap W}\), i.e: there exists a section \(\tau \in \sheaf{I}(V \cup W)\) such that \(\tau|_{V} = \tau'|_{V}\) and \(\tau'|_{W} = \tau|_{W}\).
  Clearly \(\tau\) is sent to \(\sigma|_{V \cap W}\).

  Consider now a pair \((W, \tau)\) which is maximal (for the obvious order relation), where \(W\) is an open subset of \(U\) and \(\tau\) is a section of \(\sheaf{I}\) lifting \(\sigma\) to \(W\).
  Since \(\sigma\) lifts locally on \(\sheaf{I}\) and using the previous result we see that \(W = U\).

  But then the \hyperref[thm:LES-in-cohomology]{long exact sequence in cohomology} of our sequence and the fact that for \(i > 0 \quad \cohomology{i}(X, \sheaf{I}) = 0\) imply that \(\cohomology{1}(X, \sheaf{F}) = 0\) and
  \begin{equation*}
    \forall k - 1 \geq 1 \quad \cohomology{k}(X, \sheaf{F}) = \cohomology{k-1}(X, \sheaf{G})
  \end{equation*}
  Furthermore surjectivity of the maps \(\sheaf{I}(U) \epiarrow \sheaf{G}(U)\) implies that \(\sheaf{G}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.

  It follows by induction that
  \begin{equation*}
    \forall k > 0 \quad \cohomology{k}(X, \sheaf{F}) = 0
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}


Using \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque sheaves} we can construct a canonical (and functorial) resolution of a \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules} to compute its \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology}.
\begin{construction}[Godement resolution] \label{def:godement-resolution}
  Let \(\sheaf{F}\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf-of-modules]{sheaf of \(\sheaf{O}_{X}\)-modules}.
  The \textbf{Godement resolution} of \(\sheaf{F}\) is constructed by considering the inclussion of \(\sheaf{F}\) into the \textit{Godement sheaf} \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}\)
  \begin{equation*}
    U \mapsto \bigoplus_{p \in U} \sheaf{F}_{p} \eqcolon \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(U)
  \end{equation*}
  Note that \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}\) is \hyperref[def:flasque-sheaf]{flasque}.

  We then inject the quotient \(\quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}}\) into \(\left( \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}} \right)_{\operatorname{God}}\) and so on.
\end{construction}

\begin{example}
  Let's try to compute \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} using the \hyperref[def:godement-resolution]{Godement resolution}.

  Let \(X = \{p, q\}\) with the \textit{Sierpiński} topology \(\mathcal{T} = \{\varnothing, \{p\}, X\}\).
  Consider the \hyperref[def:constant-sheaf]{constant sheaf} supported at the integers \(\sheaf{F} = \underline{\mathbb{Z}}\).

  To compute the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology} of \(\sheaf{F}\), we are going to construct the complex of global sections:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0 \arrow[r] & \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}) \arrow[r, "d^{0}"] & \Gamma\left(X, \left( \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}} \right)_{\operatorname{God}}\right) \arrow[r, "d^{1}"] & \cdots
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}

  Note that the \hyperref[def:stalk-sheaf]{stalks} of \(\sheaf{F}\) are:
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{F}_{p} = \sheaf{F}(\{p\}) = \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \sheaf{F}_{q} = \sheaf{F}(X) = \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}

  Consider the Godement sheaf associated to \(\sheaf{F}\):
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(U) = \bigoplus_{x \in U} \sheaf{F}_{x}.
  \end{equation*}
  We have that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}) = \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}(X) = \sheaf{F}_{p} \oplus \sheaf{F}_{q} = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}
  This means that we have a map \(d^{-1}\) as follows:
  \begin{equation*}
    d^{-1}: \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F})}_{\mathbb{Z}} \to \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}})}_{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}}, \quad d^{-1}(k) = (k, k).
  \end{equation*}
  Let \(\sheaf{Q}^1 = \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}}}{\sheaf{F}}\) be the \hyperref[def:categorical-cokernel]{cokernel} sheaf.
  If we compute its stalks, we have that:
  \begin{align*}
    (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{p} &= \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}, p}}{\sheaf{F}_p} = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0, \\
    (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{q} &= \quot{\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}, q}}{\sheaf{F}_q} = \quot{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}}{\langle(k, k)\rangle} \cong \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{align*}
  Therefore, the global sections of the Godement sheaf of \(\sheaf{Q}^1\) are:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma\left(X, (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\right) = (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{p} \oplus (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{q} = 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}.
  \end{equation*}
  This produces a differential map:
  \begin{equation*}
    d^{0}: \underbrace{\Gamma(X, \sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}})}_{\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}} \to \underbrace{\Gamma\left(X, (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\right)}_{\mathbb{Z}}, \quad d^{0}(m, n) = m - n.
  \end{equation*}
  Let \(\sheaf{Q}^2\) be the \hyperref[def:categorical-cokernel]{cokernel} of the map \(\sheaf{F}_{\operatorname{God}} \to (\sheaf{Q}^1)_{\operatorname{God}}\).
  If we compute its \hyperref[def:stalk-sheaf]{stalks}, we have:
  \begin{align*}
    (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{p} &= \quot{0}{0} = 0, \\
    (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{q} &= \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0.
  \end{align*}
  Therefore, we have that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \Gamma \left( X, (\sheaf{Q}^2)_{\operatorname{God}} \right) = 0 \oplus 0 = 0.
  \end{equation*}
  This means that our resolution of global sections is:
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
      0 \arrow[r] & \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r, "d^{0}", "{(m, n) \mapsto m - n}"'] & \mathbb{Z} \arrow[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Therefore, the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology groups} of \(X\) with coefficients in \(\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\) are:
  \begin{align*}
    \cohomology{0}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= \ker(d^{0}) = \{(m, n) \in \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \mid m - n = 0\} \cong \mathbb{Z}, \\
    \cohomology{1}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= \operatorname{coker}(d^{0}) = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\operatorname{im}(d^{0})} = \quot{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}} = 0, \\
    \cohomology{i}(X, \underline{\mathbb{Z}}) &= 0 \quad \forall i > 1.
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

Notice that if the Godement resolution makes computing sheaf cohomology difficult on a \textit{simple} space, it renders the task nearly impossible on more \textit{complicated} spaces.

To address this, the upcoming chapters will explore how we can select different, more efficient resolutions to compute cohomology on sufficiently nice topological spaces.
