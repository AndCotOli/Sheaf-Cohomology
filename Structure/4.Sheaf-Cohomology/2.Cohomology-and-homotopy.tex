\chapter{Cohomology and homotopy}

In this chapter, we show that sheaf cohomology is a homotopy invariant.
We prove a slightly stronger result: it is invariant by homotopy over a base space \(S\).

The main reference for this chapter is \cite[Section 2.7]{kashiwaraSheavesManifolds1990}.

These results will also be useful when computing the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology} of simple spaces as we will see later.

\begin{notation}
  We will denote \(I \coloneq [0, 1]\) the unit interval.
\end{notation}

We begin first by defining what we mean for a homotopy relative to a base space.

\begin{definition}\label{def:continuous-map-over}
  A \textbf{topological space over a base} \(S\) is a triple \((X, S, p_{X})\) where \(X\) and \(S\) are topological spaces and \(p_{X}\colon X \to S\) is a continuous map.
  
  Given \((X, S, p_{X})\) and \((Y, S, p_{Y})\) two topological spaces over the same base \(S\), we say that a continuous map \(f\colon X \to Y\) is \textbf{continuous over \(S\)} if the following diagram commutes
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      X \arrow[rr, "f"] \arrow[dr, "p_{X}"'] & & Y \arrow[dl, "p_{Y}"] \\
      & S
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
\end{definition}

Note that this relative framework is generalization of the non-relative case, since any standard topological scenario can be viewed through a relative lens simply by taking a product with the base space as demonstrated below.

\begin{example}
  Any continuous map \(f\colon X \to Y\) can be made \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous over \(S\)} trivially by considering
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      X \times S \arrow[rr, "{(f, \id_{S})}"] \arrow[dr, "p_{X}"'] & & Y \times S \arrow[dl, "p_{Y}"] \\
      & S
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  where \(p_{X}\) and \(p_{Y}\) are the obvious projections.
\end{example}

With relative maps defined, we can now define relative homotopies.
A homotopy over \(S\) is a continuous deformation where every intermediate step is itself a continuous map over \(S\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:homotopy-over}
  Let \(X, Y\) and \(S\) be topological spaces.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let \(f_{0},f_{1}\colon X \rightrightarrows Y\) be two \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous maps over \(S\)}.
          We say that \(f_{0}\) and \(f_{1}\) are \textbf{homotopic} over \(S\) if there exists a continuous map \(h\colon X \times I \to Y\) over \(S\) such that \(f_{0} = h(-, 0)\) and \(f_{1} = h(-, 1)\).
          We denote this by \(f_{0} \sim_{S} f_{1}\).
    \item A \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous maps over \(S\)} \(f\colon X \to Y\) is a \textbf{homotopy equivalence} over \(S\) if there exists a \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous maps over \(S\)} \(g\colon Y \to X\) such that \(g \circ f \sim_{S} \id_{X}\) and \(f \circ g \sim_{S} \id_{Y}\).
          In this case we write \(X \sim_{S} Y\).
    \item A topological space \(Z\) is \textbf{contractible} over \(S\) if the map \(p_{Z}\colon Z \to S\) is a homotopy equivalence.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}
  If \(S = \{\ast\}\) is the one-point topological space we recover the usual definition of homotopy of topological spaces.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
  If \(f_{0}, f_{1}\colon X \rightrightarrows Y\) are \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopic over \(S\)} we have a commutative diagram
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      X \arrow[d, "j_{i}"'] \arrow[dr, "f_{t}"] & \\
      X \times I \arrow[d, "p"'] \arrow[r, "h"] & Y \arrow[d, "p_{Y}"] \\
      X \arrow[r, "p_{X}"'] & S
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  where \(j_{t}(z) = (z, t)\) for \(t \in \{0, 1\}\).
\end{remark}

Because \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopies} are parameterized by the unit interval, we must understand how \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} behaves on \(I\) before establishing the global invariance theorem.

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:vanishing-cohomology-interval}
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{I})\). Then
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For \(i > 1\) we have \(\cohomology{i}(I; \sheaf{F}) = 0\).
    \item If \(\sheaf{F}(I) \to \sheaf{F}_{t}\) is an \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{epimorphism} for all \(t \in I\) then \(\cohomology{1}(I; \sheaf{F}) = 0\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(j \geq 1\) and \(s \in \cohomology{j}(I; \sheaf{F})\).
  We want to show that \(s = 0\).

  For \(0 \leq t_{1} < t_{2} \leq 1\) consider the morphism
  \begin{equation*}
    f_{t_{1}, t_{2}}: \cohomology{i}(I; \sheaf{F}) \to \cohomology{i}([t_{1}, t_{2}]; \sheaf{F}|_{[t_{1}, t_{2}]})
  \end{equation*}
  and let
  \begin{equation*}
    J \coloneq \{t \in [0, 1] \mid f_{0, t}(s) = 0\}
  \end{equation*}

  \hyperref[ex:cohomology-over-point]{Since \(\cohomology{j}(\{0\}, \sheaf{F}) = 0\)} we have that \(0 \in J\).
  Furthermore if \(t \in J\) then for any \(t' \leq t\) the map \(f_{0, t'}\) factors as
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \cohomology{i}(I; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r, "f_{0, t'}"] \arrow[d, "f_{0, t}"'] & \cohomology{i}([0, t']; \sheaf{F}) \\
      \cohomology{i}([0, t]; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[ur]
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{center}
  Therefore since \(f_{0, t}(s) = 0\) we have that \(f_{0, t'}(s) = 0\), i.e: \([0, t] \subseteq J\) and thus \(J\) is a sub-interval of \([0, 1]\) starting at 0.

  To prove that \(J = [0, 1]\) we will see that it is both open and closed.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[Open] Suppose \(t_{0} \in J\). By \autoref{lemma:cohomology-compact-subsets} we have that
          \begin{equation*}
            \cohomology{j}([0, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \cong \varinjlim_{t > t_{0}} \cohomology{j}([0, t]; \sheaf{F})
          \end{equation*}
          Therefore there exists \([0, t_{0} + \varepsilon[\) such that \(f_{0, t}(s) = 0\) for all \(t \in [0, t_{0} + \varepsilon[\), and thus \(J\) is open.
    \item[Closed] Let \(t_{0} \coloneq \operatorname{sup} J\). We want to see that \(t_{0} \in J\).
          For \(t \in [0, t_{0}]\) consider the \hyperref[thm:mayer-vietoris-closed]{Mayer-Vietoris sequence} associated with \([0, t_{0}] = [0, t] \cup [t, t_{0}]\):
          \begin{center}
            \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
              \cdots \arrow[r] & \cohomology{i}([0, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{i}([0, t]; \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{i}([t, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{i}(\{t\}, \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cdots
            \end{tikzcd}
          \end{center}
          If \(i > 1\) then since \(\cohomology{i}(\{t\}; \sheaf{F}) = \cohomology{i-1}(\{t\}; \sheaf{F}) = 0\) we have an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism}
          \begin{equation*}
            \cohomology{i}([0, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{i}([0, t]; \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{i}([t, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F})
          \end{equation*}
          Similarly, if \(\sheaf{F}(I) \epiarrow \sheaf{F}_{t}\) is an \hyperref[def:mono-epi-morphism]{epimorphism} we have
          \begin{center}
            \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
              \cohomology{0}([0, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r, two heads] & \cohomology{0}(\{t\}; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r] & \cohomology{1}([0, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \arrow[r, "\sim"] &  \cohomology{1}([0, t]; \sheaf{F}) \oplus \cohomology{1}([t, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F})
            \end{tikzcd}
          \end{center}

          In both cases for \(f_{0, t_{0}}(s)\) to be zero it suffices for its components in \(\cohomology{i}([0, t]; \sheaf{F})\) and \(\cohomology{i}([t, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F})\) to vanish.

          On the one hand since \(t < t_{0} = \operatorname{sup} J\) we can choose \(t \in J\), so \(f_{0, t}(s) = 0\).
          On the other by \autoref{lemma:cohomology-compact-subsets} we have
          \begin{equation*}
            \varinjlim_{t < t_{0}} \cohomology{i}([t, t_{0}]; \sheaf{F}) \cong \cohomology{i}(\{t_{0}\}; \sheaf{F}) = 0
          \end{equation*}
          Therefore \(f_{0, t_{0}}(s) = 0\) and thus \(t_{0} \in J\).
  \end{itemize}

  Since \(J\) is non-empty, open and closed and \([0, 1]\) is connected necessarily \(J = [0, 1]\).
  Therefore \(s = f_{0, 1}(s) = 0\).
\end{proof}

We also need a mechanism to compare sheaves across different spaces over \(S\).
Given a relative map \(f: X \to Y\), we cannot directly compare the derived functors \(\operatorname{R}^i p_{X,*}\) and \(\operatorname{R}^i p_{Y,*}\) because they act on different \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf categories} (\(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_X)\) and \(\Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_Y)\) respectively).
To remedy this, we use the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse image functor} \(p^{-1}\) to pull coefficients up from the base space \(S\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:induced-map-over}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous map} over \(S\).

  For all \(i \geq 0\) there exists a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural transformation}
  \begin{equation*}
    f^{\#, i} \coloneq \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{Y, \ast} \circ p_{Y}^{-1} \Rightarrow \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1}
  \end{equation*}
  where the \hyperref[def:functor]{functors} are
  \begin{align*}
    \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1}\colon& \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S}) \to \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S}) \\
    \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{Y, \ast} \circ p_{Y}^{-1}\colon& \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S}) \to \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S})
  \end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S})\) be a \hyperref[def:sheaf]{sheaf} over \(S\).

  Consider an \hyperref[def:resolution]{injective resolution} \(p_{Y}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \to \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\) of the \hyperref[def:inverse-image-sheaf]{inverse image} \(p_{Y}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{Y})\)
  Using the \hyperref[def:unit-counit]{unit of the adjunction} \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction-sheaf]{\(f_{\ast} \dashv f^{-1}\)} we get a natural map of \hyperref[def:complex]{complexes} in \(Y\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \to \left( f_{\ast} \circ f^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  Applying the \hyperref[def:direct-image-sheaf]{direct image} \(p_{Y, \ast}\) to this, since \(p_{Y} \circ f = p_{X}\) we get a \hyperref[def:complex]{morphism of complexes} in \(S\)
   \begin{equation*}
    p_{Y, \ast}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}) \to \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ f^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  Note that, since \hyperref[cor:inverse-image-sheaf-exact]{\(f^{-1}\) is exact}, \(f^{-1}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet})\) is an \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact resolution} of \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F})\).
  However, it need not be made of \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injectives}.
  To fix this, consider a proper \hyperref[def:resolution]{injective resolution} \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\).
  By \autoref{thm:existence-of-lifting} there exists a morphism (unique up to \hyperref[def:homotopy]{homotopy of complexes}) lifting the identity
  \begin{equation*}
    f^{-1}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}
  \end{equation*}
  Applying \(p_{X, \ast}\) we get a morphism in \(S\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ f^{-1} \right) \left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right) \to p_{X, \ast}\left(\sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\right)
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore composing with the previous map we get
  \begin{equation*}
    p_{Y, \ast}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}) \to \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ f^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to p_{X, \ast} \left( \sheaf{J}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  Applying the \hyperref[def:right-derived-functor]{\(i\)-th right derived functor} we get a map
   \begin{equation*}
    \left(\operatorname{R}^{i}p_{Y, \ast} \circ p_{Y}^{-1}\right)(\sheaf{F}) \to \left(\operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right)(\sheaf{F}) \qedhere
  \end{equation*} 
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  If \(S = \{\ast\}\) then \hyperref[ex:one-point-sheaves-are-category]{\(\Sh_{\ast}(\category{C}) \simeq \category{C}\)} and \(p_{Z}^{-1} \underline{M} = \underline{M}\) for all \(M \in \category{C}\) and \(Z \in \{X, Y\}\).

  In this case the morphism of \autoref{lemma:induced-map-over} is
  \begin{equation*}
    f^{\#, i}(\underline{M})\colon \cohomology{i}(Y; \underline{M}) \to \cohomology{i}(X; \underline{M})
  \end{equation*}
  We denote this map by \(f^{\ast}\). 
\end{remark}

We now arrive at the central result of this chapter where we show that \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{relative homotopies} preserve \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology}.
This means that continuously deforming a map over \(S\) does not alter its effect on the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{cohomology} of sheaves pulled up from \(S\).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:homotopy-invariance-cohomology}
  Let \(f_{0}, f_{1}\colon X \rightrightarrows Y\) be two \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopic maps} over \(S\).
  Then \(f_{0}^{\#, i} = f_{1}^{\#, i}\) for all \(i \geq 0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sheaf{F} \in \Sh_{S}(\category{C})\).
  Choose \hyperref[def:resolution]{injective resolutions} \(p_{Y}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \to \sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\) and \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\).

  Since \(f_{t} = h \circ j_{t}\) we can factor \(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \to \left( f_{t, \ast} \circ f_{t}^{-1} \right)(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet})\) through
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \to \left( h_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to \left(\left( h \circ j_{t} \right)_{\ast} \circ \left( h \circ j_{t} \right)^{-1}\right) \left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right)
  \end{equation*}
  Pushing this factorization via \(p_{Y, \ast}\) we get
  \begin{equation*}
     p_{Y, \ast} \left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right) \to \left( p_{Y, \ast} \circ h_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to \left(p_{Y, \ast} \circ \left( h \circ j_{t} \right)_{\ast} \circ \left( h \circ j_{t} \right)^{-1}\right) \left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right)
  \end{equation*}
  On the one hand since \(p_{Y} \circ h = p_{X} \circ p\) the middle term becomes
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( p_{Y, \ast} \circ h_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) = \left( \left( p_{Y} \circ h \right)_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) = \left( \left( p_{X} \circ p \right)_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  On the other since \(f_{t} = h \circ j_{t}\) and \(p_{Y} \circ f_{t} = p_{X}\) we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \left(p_{Y, \ast} \circ \left( h \circ j_{t} \right)_{\ast} \circ \left( h \circ j_{t} \right)^{-1}\right) \left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right) = \left( \left( p_{Y} \circ f_{t} \right)_{\ast} \circ f_{t}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) = \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ f_{t}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  As in \autoref{lemma:induced-map-over} since \(f_{t}^{-1}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet})\) is an \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact} (but not necessarilly \hyperref[def:injective-projective-object]{injective}) \hyperref[def:resolution]{resolution} of \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F})\) there exists a unique (up to homotopy) lifting of the identity
  \begin{equation*}
    f_{t}^{-1}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}
  \end{equation*}
  where \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F}) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\) is an \hyperref[def:injective-resolution]{injective resolution} of \(p_{X}^{-1}(\sheaf{F})\).
  Applying \(p_{X, \ast}\) we obtain a map
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ f_{t}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to p_{X, \ast} \left( \sheaf{J}^{\bullet} \right)
  \end{equation*}

  Therefore we obtain a factorization of \(f^{\#, t}\)
  \begin{equation*}
    p_{Y, \ast}\left(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet}\right) \xrightarrow{g_{1}} \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \xrightarrow{g_{2}} p_{X, \ast}\left(\sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\right)
  \end{equation*}
  Denote by
  \begin{equation*}
    \alpha_{t} \coloneq \cohomology{\bullet}g_{2}\colon \cohomology{\bullet} \left( \left( p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \right) \to \cohomology{\bullet} \left( p_{X, \ast}\left(\sheaf{J}^{\bullet}\right) \right)
  \end{equation*}
  the induced map on cohomology of \(g_{2}\).
  Since \(g_{1}\) does not depend on \(t\), we only need to see that \(\alpha_{0} = \alpha_{1}\).
  
  The map \(\alpha_{t}\) is induced by
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( p_{\ast} \circ h^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to \underbrace{\left( j_{t}^{-1} \circ h^{-1} \right)}_{f_{t}^{-1}} \left( \sheaf{I}^{\bullet} \right) \to \sheaf{J}^{\bullet}
  \end{equation*}
  Note that \(h^{-1}(\sheaf{I}^{\bullet})\) is an \hyperref[def:exact-sequence]{exact sequence} \hyperref[def:resolution]{resolving} \(\left( h^{-1} \circ p_{Y}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right) \cong \left( p^{-1} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right)\).
  Since by \autoref{lemma:vanishing-cohomology-interval} higher cohomology (\(k > 0\)) vanishes, the cohomology of \(\alpha_{t}\) is concentrated in degree zero, which is
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( p_{\ast} \circ p^{-1} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right) \to \left( j_{t}^{-1} \circ p^{-1} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right)
  \end{equation*}

  However, note that we have \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphisms}
  \begin{equation*}
    p_{X}^{-1} \left( \sheaf{F} \right) \isoarrow \left( p_{\ast} \circ p^{-1} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right) \isoarrow \left( j_{t}^{-1} \circ p^{-1} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right) \isoarrow p_{X}^{-1} \left( \sheaf{F} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  whose composition is the identity on \(p_{X}^{-1}\), which means that \(\alpha_{t}\) is induced by the identity.
  Indeed:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The first one comes from the \hyperref[def:unit-counit]{unit of the adjunction} \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction]{\(p^{-1} \dashv p_{\ast}\)}.
    \item Applying \(p_{\ast}\) to the \hyperref[def:unit-counit]{unit of the adjunction} \hyperref[thm:direct-inverse-image-adjunction]{\(j_{t}^{-1} \dashv j_{t, \ast}\)} we get
          \begin{equation*}
            p_{\ast} \isoarrow p_{\ast} \circ j_{t, \ast} \circ j_{t}^{-1} = \left( p \circ j_{t} \right)_{\ast} \circ j_{t}^{-1} = \id_{\ast} \circ j_{t}^{-1} = j_{t}^{-1}
          \end{equation*}
    \item The last one is direct from the \hyperref[lemma:composition-inverse-image-sheaf]{composition of the inverse image}
          \begin{equation*}
            j_{t}^{-1} \circ p^{-1} = \left( p \circ j_{t} \right)^{-1} = \id^{-1} = \id
          \end{equation*}
  \end{itemize}

  Therefore since \(\alpha_{t}\) does not depend on \(t\) we get \(\alpha_{0} = \alpha_{1}\) and thus \(f_{0}^{\#, i} = f_{1}^{\#, i}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cohomology-and-homotopy-equivalence}
  Let \(f\colon X \to Y\) be a \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopy equivalence} over \(S\).
  Then \(f^{\#, i}\) is a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural isomorphism} for all \(i \geq 0\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{definition} there exists a \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous map over \(S\)} \(g\colon Y \to X\) such that \(g \circ f \sim_{S} \id_{X}\) and \(f \circ g \sim_{S} \id_{Y}\).

  Since \(f^{\#, i}\) is a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural transformation} we have
  \begin{equation*}
    \left( g \circ f \right)^{\#, i} = f^{\#, i} \circ g^{\#, i} \qquad \left( f \circ g \right)^{\#, i} = g^{\#, i} \circ f^{\#, i}
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore since \hyperref[thm:homotopy-invariance-cohomology]{homotopic maps induce the same transformation}
  \begin{equation*}
    f^{\#, i} \circ g^{\#, i} = (\id_{X})^{\#, i} = \id \qquad g^{\#, i} \circ f^{\#, i} = \left( \id_{Y} \right)^{\#, i} = \id \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}

We end this chapter by giving some results that will help us compute the \hyperref[def:sheaf-cohomology]{sheaf cohomology} of simple spaces in the next chapter.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cohomology-and-contractible}
  Let \(X\) be \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{contractible over \(S\)}.
  Then for all \(\sheaf{F} \in \Mod(\underline{\mathbf{k}}_{S})\) the \hyperref[def:unit-counit]{adjunction unit map}
  \begin{equation*}
    \sheaf{F} \to \left(\operatorname{R}^{0}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right)
  \end{equation*}
  is an \hyperref[def:categorical-isomorphism]{isomorphism} and for all \(i > 0\)
  \begin{equation*}
    \left(\operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \right) \left( \sheaf{F} \right) = 0
  \end{equation*}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  We can view \(S\) as a space over itself via the identity map \(\id_{S}\colon S \to S\).
  By hypothesis the map \(p_{X}\colon X \to S\) is a \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopy equivalence over \(S\)}.
  Therefore by \autoref{cor:cohomology-and-homotopy-equivalence} \(p_{X}^{\#, i}\) is a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural isomorphism}
  \begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{R}^{i}\id_{S, \ast} \circ \id_{S}^{-1} \isoarrow \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1}
  \end{equation*}

  For \(i = 0\) we have \(\operatorname{R}^{0}\id_{S, \ast} = \id\).
  Therefore
  \begin{equation*}
    \id_{S} \isoarrow \operatorname{R}^{0}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1}
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand since \(\id_{S, \ast}\) is \hyperref[def:exact-functor]{exact} \(\operatorname{R}^{i}\id_{S, \ast} = 0\) for all \(i > 0\).
  Therefore
  \begin{equation*}
    0 \isoarrow \operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \qedhere
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:deformation-retract}
  Let \(X\) be a topological space over \(S\) with \(p_{X}\colon X \to S\).
  Let \(i\colon A \hookrightarrow X\) be a subspace with \(p_{A} \coloneq p_{X}|_{A}\).

  We say that \(A\) is a \textbf{deformation retract} of \(X\) over \(S\) if there exists a \hyperref[def:continuous-map-over]{continuous map over \(S\)} \(r\colon X \to A\) with \(r \circ i = \id_{A}\) and a \hyperref[def:homotopy-over]{homotopy over \(S\)} \(F\colon X \times I \to A\) between \(\id_{X}\) and \(i \circ r\).
  The map \(r\) is called the \textbf{retraction} of \(X\) into \(A\).

  If \(F(a, t) = a\) for all \(t \in I\) and \(a \in A\), we say that \(A\) is a \textbf{strong deformation retract} of \(X\) over \(S\).
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cohomology-and-retraction}
  Let \(i\colon A \hookrightarrow X\) be a \hyperref[def:deformation-retract]{strong deformation retract} of X over S.
  Then the induced map
  \begin{equation*}
    i^{\#, n} \colon \operatorname{R}^{n}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1} \to \operatorname{R}^{n}p_{A, \ast} \circ p_{A}^{-1}
  \end{equation*}
  is a \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural isomorphism} for all \(n \geq 0\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Let \(r\colon X \to A\) be the \hyperref[def:deformation-retract]{retraction} of \(X\) into \(A\).
  Since \(r \circ i = \id_{A}\) we have for all \(n \geq 0\)
  \begin{equation*}
    i^{\#, n} \circ r^{\#, n} = \left( i \circ r \right)^{\#, n} = \id_{A}^{\#, n} = \id_{\operatorname{R}^{i}p_{A, \ast} \circ p_{A}^{-1}}
  \end{equation*}
  On the other hand since \(i \circ r \sim_{S} \operatorname{id}_{X}\) by \autoref{thm:homotopy-invariance-cohomology} we have
  \begin{equation*}
    r^{\#, n} \circ i^{\#, n} = \left( r \circ i \right)^{\#, n} = \id_{X}^{\#, n} = \id_{\operatorname{R}^{i}p_{X, \ast} \circ p_{X}^{-1}}
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore \(r^{\#, n}\) and \(i^{\#, n}\) are inverses of each other and thus \hyperref[def:natural-transformation]{natural isomorphisms}.
\end{proof}
